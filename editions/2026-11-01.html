Google Lost $500 Billion in a Day. Hackers Stole $6.3 Billion in Crypto. And the Fed Isn’t Done Yet.

It was a big week for markets, money and security, two days before Americans head to the polls. Here is what you need to know.

01 / The Headlines

The main stories.

Google’s Parent Just Had Its Worst Day Ever — $500 Billion Gone

Google's parent fell nearly 13% on Thursday, wiping out roughly $500 billion in market value. Investors balked after heavy spending on AI infrastructure pushed the company's free cash flow into negative territory for the quarter.

Source: CNBC, Fortune

The Economy Is Growing Fast. So Why Does Everyone Feel Broke?

U.S. growth sped up from July through September, powered by consumer spending and business investment. The strong number arrived days before the midterms, while many households say high prices still make it feel like a much weaker economy.

Source: Axios

The Fed Held Rates — But Two Officials Wanted to Raise Them

The Federal Reserve kept its benchmark rate at 3.75% to 4% in a 10-2 vote. Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissented in favor of raising rates. With inflation still running hot, all eyes are on the December meeting.

Source: Fortune, Axios

Meta’s Revenue Surged 26% — But Its AI Spending Is Out of Control

Meta reported quarterly revenue of $64.6 billion, up 26%, and net income that jumped 264%. But investors are watching costs: the company's heavy AI infrastructure spending pushed free cash flow into negative territory. CEO Mark Zuckerberg defended the bets, saying AI is reshaping every product at Meta.

Source: ABC News, Times of Oman

He Left in 1998. He Just Finished Walking Around the World.

British adventurer Karl Bushby completed his 28-year walk around the world, greeted by his mother at the finish line. He set out from southern South America and walked through Central America, across a frozen strait to Russia, through Asia and Europe, and finally back to Britain. He called it the longest unbroken walk in history.

Source: PBS NewsHour

02 / The Context

The background.

$6.3 Billion Stolen in Hours: Inside the Biggest Crypto Heist Ever

What happened

Hackers moved about $6.3 billion out of Binance, the world's largest crypto exchange. Blockchain intelligence firms tracing the money say it's very likely the work of TraderTraitor, a North Korean state-backed hacking group.

How we got here

North Korea has spent years targeting crypto companies. The previous record, a roughly $1.5 billion hack of the exchange Bybit in 2025, was also pinned on North Korean hackers by the FBI.

Why it matters

Stolen crypto is a major source of money for North Korea's government, which is under heavy international sanctions. A theft this size also raises fresh questions about how safe customer funds are on even the biggest exchanges.

What to watch

Investigators are racing to trace and freeze the funds before they're laundered. Watch for official attribution from U.S. law enforcement and any new security rules for exchanges.

Source: NPR, CBS News, NBC News
