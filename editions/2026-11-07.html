Miller Will Replace Wiles as Trump’s Chief of Staff

President Donald Trump has chosen Stephen Miller to replace Susie Wiles as White House chief of staff. ABC reports the change takes effect Monday, following Republican losses in the congressional elections.

Miller already serves as deputy chief of staff for policy and helped shape Trump’s immigration agenda. AP describes his longstanding role in the president’s inner circle, dating to the 2016 campaign.

Wiles will move to Trump’s political operation rather than leave his orbit entirely. AP reports that Trump praised her work, while Wiles said her commitment to the president and his agenda would continue.

Why it matters. The appointment puts a policy architect in charge of the White House staff before Democrats take congressional control. AP reports that Miller brings extensive knowledge of executive power and federal government.

Hiring Adds 165,000 Jobs as Unemployment Falls

U.S. employers added 165,000 jobs in October, exceeding the 65,000 increase economists expected. Reuters reports that unemployment fell to 4.1%, from 4.2% in September, in Friday’s Labor Department release.

The report also revised the recent hiring picture upward. Reuters says August and September added 84,000 more jobs than previously estimated, showing that the labor market was stronger than earlier figures suggested.

The unemployment rate declined even as more people entered the labor force. The report also showed annual wage growth of 3.1%, leaving policymakers to assess employment strength alongside continuing pressure on household budgets.

Why it matters. The figures give the Federal Reserve another measure of economic resilience before its next policy decision. Reuters notes that officials are also monitoring inflation linked to the Middle East conflict.

New Kyiv Strikes Damage Homes Before Planned Talks

Russian missile strikes hit Kyiv early Saturday, damaging residential buildings, according to city officials. Reuters reports that debris struck an apartment building, while authorities warned residents that further attacks remained possible.

The city’s military administration also reported damage to a building housing a kindergarten. Al Jazeera reports that officials described injuries, with the attacks following President Volodymyr Zelenskyy’s warning about threats to civilian infrastructure.

Ukrainian, Russian and U.S. negotiators are expected in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday. Reuters says the talks seek a resolution to the war, after the previous round took place in February.

Why it matters. The attacks show the continuing risks to civilians while diplomats discuss security arrangements. Al Jazeera reports that Ukraine is seeking specific guarantees about how support from Western allies would work after a settlement.

A $1.3 Billion Engine Deal Expands AST’s Launch Plans

Firefly Aerospace will supply engines for AST SpaceMobile’s planned Blacklift rocket under a deal worth more than $1.3 billion. Reuters reports that the agreement was announced Friday, with manufacturing rights also included.

The initial agreement covers 27 Miranda engines and nine Vira engines for three rockets. AST says the vehicle could carry five BlueBird satellites, with a first launch targeted for late 2028.

The company wants more control over deployment of its cellular broadband satellite network. Reuters describes an effort to reduce reliance on outside rockets, although AST says it will continue using other launch providers.

Why it matters. Access to launch capacity is becoming part of the competition to deliver satellite services. Reuters reports that the deal also funds Firefly production; the proposed rocket’s schedule remains a target, not a completed launch.

Why New Zealand’s Election Count May Not Settle Its Government

Part 1

New Zealand’s two largest parties were closely matched in reporting checked at 11:14:36 UTC. Al Jazeera reports that National and Labour each had just under 30% with more than 80% counted. The governing coalition held a narrow projected advantage, but preliminary results do not establish a final government or settle every seat.

Part 2

The contest began with a close early count. Reuters reported a narrow National lead in the first results. New Zealand’s electoral system gives voters a local candidate choice and a separate party choice. The party vote helps determine parliamentary representation, making the performance of smaller parties important to any eventual governing arrangement.

Part 3

Household costs were central to the campaign. Al Jazeera describes grocery prices and supermarket competition as major issues. National promised fiscal discipline and changes to the pension scheme. Labour proposed cheaper healthcare and transport. The centrist Opportunity Party crossed the parliamentary threshold in preliminary reporting, creating another potential participant in negotiations over governing support.

Part 4

Final results are expected Nov. 27, including ballots cast by New Zealanders overseas. Al Jazeera reports that government formation has no fixed deadline. Former minister Peter Dunne told the outlet that negotiations could take several weeks. Parliament must sit by early January, leaving the completed count and any talks over governing support ahead.
