White Declined Trump’s Press Secretary Offer, CNN Reports

President Donald Trump publicly offered UFC chief Dana White the White House press secretary job Saturday. CNN reports that White declined, citing a source familiar with the matter. Reuters also carried that attributed report.

One White House aide told CNN that Trump was joking about the offer. CNN reports that White deflected a question about it at a news conference. He said he would meet Trump in Washington on Monday.

White and Trump have worked together since UFC events at Trump’s casino in the early 2000s. Reuters reports that White publicly supported Trump’s 2016 campaign. Trump hosted UFC fights at the White House this summer.

Why it matters. Trump is still seeking a chief spokesperson. Reuters reports that White and UFC’s parent company did not immediately answer requests for comment. The position has been vacant since Katie Zacharia’s brief tenure ended last month.

Tesla Adds a Cybertruck Shift Before a Planned Factory Change

Tesla will add a production shift to try to finish outstanding Cybertruck orders before dismantling the line next year. Reuters reports that Elon Musk announced the extra shift Saturday. The change remains a production plan.

Musk said the order backlog exceeds what Tesla can make before a planned Cybercab expansion around June or July next year. Reuters reports that the Texas factory will shift resources toward the robotaxi.

The Cybercab is a two seat vehicle central to Musk’s autonomous driving ambitions. Reuters reports that U.S. Cybertruck sales fell to about 20,000 last year, from nearly 39,000 in 2024, citing Kelley Blue Book.

Why it matters. The added shift is intended to clear existing orders before factory capacity changes. Reuters describes a broader move toward AI and robotics. The pickup has faced repeated recalls and quality concerns since launch.

Poland Begins Mourning as Investigators Examine the Krakow Attack

Poland began two days of national mourning Sunday after a gunman killed six people in Krakow’s Main Square. AP reports that the square remained partly closed Sunday morning. Police shot and detained the suspected attacker.

Authorities confirmed that the suspect is a 24 year old Polish man. Reuters reports that the interior minister cited possible influence from extreme nationalist ideology. Investigators have not completed their account of the attack.

Police also found three bodies at the suspect’s family home in another city. AP reports that police identified them as his parents and sister. Officials praised the officers who stopped the attacker; both remain hospitalized.

Why it matters. The investigation covers a public attack and deaths at a separate home. Reuters reports that Poland’s security agencies are assisting. Authorities are examining the suspect’s background, weapons and possible ideological influences.

Eisenkot Gets the First Chance to Form Israel’s Next Government

Israeli President Isaac Herzog has chosen opposition leader Gadi Eisenkot to try to form a government. AP reports that the decision followed the Oct. 27 election. Neither main political bloc won a parliamentary majority.

Eisenkot is a former military chief and a rival of Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. ABC reports that factions representing 67 lawmakers recommended him. Recommendations do not themselves establish an agreement to support a governing coalition.

His prospective partners disagree over cooperation with Arab led parties and other issues. AP describes possible coalition and minority government arrangements. Netanyahu’s Likud remained the largest individual party, leaving him an important participant in negotiations.

Why it matters. Eisenkot must negotiate across a divided parliament to secure a governing majority. ABC reports that he initially has 28 days to assemble a coalition. The president can extend that period by another 14 days.

Why Gaza’s First Heavy Rains Have Become a Shelter Emergency

Part 1

Heavy rain flooded tents across Gaza, leaving displaced families with soaked bedding and damaged shelters. Reuters reports that the Hamas run media office counted at least 70,000 flooded tents Saturday. Civil defense officials said they received 1,500 distress calls from residents. The service reported three abandoned houses collapsing, without injuries from those collapses.

Part 2

The response is constrained by more than rainfall. Municipal and civil defense officials described fuel shortages and damaged equipment that prevented an adequate response. Reuters reports that families tried reinforcing tents with sand and removing water themselves. Many residents have little shelter beyond tents after extensive destruction during the war.

Part 3

Hamas accused Israel of restricting shelters and construction materials. Israel’s aid coordination agency did not immediately answer those accusations. Reuters reports that COGAT said it had increased sanitation preparations before the storm. The agency said four additional vacuum trucks entered Gaza that week, joining eight delivered over the previous month.

Part 4

The emergency also shows how little reconstruction has advanced since last year’s ceasefire. Reuters reports that negotiators have not agreed on Hamas disarmament or Israeli troop withdrawal. Rain therefore reaches families still living in temporary camps rather than repaired homes. The immediate needs include dry bedding, usable shelter, fuel and equipment to remove floodwater.
