Anthropic Prices a Record $81 Billion IPO Ahead of Its Nasdaq Debut

Anthropic priced its initial public offering at $83 per share on Wednesday, above its expected range. CNBC reports the AI company sold about 980 million shares and raised $81.3 billion. That tops the record SpaceX set in June.

The company had planned to sell 825 million shares at $70 to $78 each. Al Jazeera reports the offering values Anthropic at roughly $1.5 trillion. Shares are set to begin trading on the Nasdaq on Thursday under the ticker ANT.

A group of tech firms including Google, Microsoft and Nvidia agreed to invest $26 billion at the IPO price, according to CNBC. Anthropic said it had $33.24 billion in revenue in the first nine months of the year. It also reported a $104 billion net loss for that period.

Why it matters. The listing is seen as a test of high AI valuations after a shaky month for tech stocks. Al Jazeera reports the Nasdaq Composite has lost 7.6 percent over the past month. CNBC reported Nasdaq-100 futures up 2.11% early Thursday, before the open.

House Democrats Nominate Jeffries to Be the Next Speaker

House Democrats nominated Rep. Hakeem Jeffries for speaker on Wednesday. NBC News reports he won a closed-door, secret-ballot vote 251 to 11, according to two sources in the room. No challenger emerged.

The full House will vote on the speaker when the new Congress starts on Jan. 3. NPR reports Democrats are poised to control at least 257 seats. If elected, Jeffries would be the first Black lawmaker to hold the job.

Jeffries focused on the cost of groceries, gas, housing and health care. He also said Democrats would work to hold officials accountable. Seven of the 11 members who voted no issued a joint statement listing progressive priorities, NBC News reported.

Why it matters. The vote sets up a Democratic House for the next two years of the Trump presidency. Jeffries told reporters the party’s job is to fight MAGA extremism, not each other. A large majority gives him room to absorb some dissent, NPR reports.

Britain Pledges 1,000 Long-Range Strike Drones for Ukraine

Britain says it will give Ukraine 1,000 long-range strike drones to hit targets beyond the front line. BBC News reports the drones are part of a new £260m package for the winter. Prime Minister Andy Burnham is set to announce it in Berlin on Thursday.

The government said the drones can fly up to 1,000km and carry heavier warheads. That would let Ukraine target military production sites, the BBC reports. The UK is also building maritime, logistics and surveillance drones for Kyiv.

Burnham is co-hosting a Berlin meeting of the Coalition of the Willing, a group of Ukraine's allies. Al Jazeera reports more than 30 leaders are expected, including Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy. Media reports say U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner are expected to meet Ukrainian and European negotiators there.

Why it matters. The pledge comes as Russia targets Ukraine's power supply before winter. Burnham said plunging Ukrainians into freezing cold will not help Putin win the war. The government said 80% of the package will be spent in the UK, the BBC reports.

Israeli Forces Cross Gaza's Yellow Line for the First Time Since the Truce

Israeli tanks pushed into Hamas-controlled central Gaza on Wednesday, beyond the so-called Yellow Line. The Guardian reports it was the first ground operation across that boundary since the October 2025 ceasefire. Dozens of displaced Palestinians fled their tents.

BBC News reports the Israeli military said the operation aimed to dismantle a terror tunnel. On Thursday morning, the Israeli agency Cogat said the activity had ended and the Salah al-Din road had reopened. It is Gaza's main north to south route.

Hamas called the incursion a flagrant violation of the ceasefire. Palestinian medics said an Israeli airstrike near the raid killed one person, NBC News reports. Medics said two other people were killed elsewhere in Gaza.

Why it matters. The operation puts new strain on a truce that has not stopped near-daily Israeli strikes. Gaza's Hamas-run health ministry says at least 1,658 people have been killed in Israeli attacks since the ceasefire began. The U.S.-backed Board of Peace has accused both Israel and Hamas of failing to honor their commitments.

What Came Out of the First Trump, Xi and Putin Meeting

What happened

President Donald Trump, China's Xi Jinping and Russia's Vladimir Putin held their first three-way meeting on Thursday in Shenzhen, China. BBC News reports the talks lasted about 90 minutes. Reuters reports they discussed the war in Ukraine and nuclear arms control. Trump has since left China.

Why it matters

After the talks, the three countries issued a joint declaration on peace. The BBC reports it says a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought. The leaders also pledged to uphold strategic stability and to raise the supply of refined petroleum products. The statement says the International Criminal Court must not abuse its jurisdiction.

What each side says

The meeting brought together the leaders of the world's biggest nuclear powers. Trump said they were really making history. Xi said the talks had groundbreaking significance, and Putin said the three share a joint responsibility for peace, the BBC reported. Before the talks, Trump met Xi and Putin separately and called both of them friends.

What comes next

The three countries remain divided on Ukraine, Iran, nuclear weapons and trade. Analysts say the gathering is unlikely to produce major agreements, Reuters reports. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Ukraine was not the main topic. The BBC reports frustration in Europe that Xi does not press Russia harder over Ukraine.
