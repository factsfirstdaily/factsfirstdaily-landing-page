Trump Demands a Probe After GDP Is Revised Up to 4.3%

President Trump on Thursday called an upward GDP revision a record-setting error and said it should be investigated, CNBC reports. He made the claim in a Thanksgiving post on Truth Social.

The Commerce Department on Wednesday revised third-quarter growth to a 4.3% annualized rate, from a 3.4% advance estimate, CNBC reports. Trump suggested the earlier figure, released before the midterm elections, was fake. He did not present any evidence, CBS News reports.

CNBC reports the revision is far from an all-time record. It was the largest since the second quarter of 2020. It tied for 16th among such revisions since 1990. CNBC says the update came on its previously announced schedule.

Why it matters. The department regularly updates its data to improve accuracy, including GDP and monthly jobs figures, CBS reports. A White House spokesman told CNBC the revision shows Trump's policies are delivering results. Traders are pricing in a nearly 100% chance of a Fed rate hike in December, CNBC reports.

Black Friday Shoppers Hunt for Value as Prices Bite

Retailers expect robust holiday spending through Black Friday despite economic headwinds, CNBC reports. Participation in Black Friday sales is expected to reach a 10-year high, according to a Deloitte survey cited by CNBC.

October consumer prices rose a seasonally adjusted 0.4%, higher than expected, CNBC reports. October retail sales grew at their slowest pace in nearly a year, according to the CNBC/NRF Retail Monitor. Walmart and Lowe's have reported slower sales and smaller baskets.

The Conference Board estimates planned holiday spending at $1,049 on average, up 5.9% from last year, CNBC reports. Adobe found shoppers spent $82.5 billion online in the first 22 days of November. Salesforce expects $84 billion in U.S. online sales this week.

Why it matters. Black Friday is the unofficial start of the holiday shopping season, though many retailers began discounts weeks ago, Al Jazeera reports. Al Jazeera says household budgets are increasingly stretched. Holiday sales are expected to top $1 trillion, though inflation boosts that growth, CNBC reports.

Strike in Kuwait Kills Two Italian Technicians

Two Italian nationals died after a strike on a facility in Kuwait that both countries blamed on Iran, the BBC reports. The pair were technicians for German arms maker Rheinmetall, Italy's foreign ministry said.

Kuwait said the two were killed by falling debris following Iranian attacks, CNBC reports. Kuwait's defense ministry earlier said drones from Iran were detected and intercepted, the BBC reports. Rheinmetall said the men were supporting air defense systems.

Iran denied responsibility, the BBC reports. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi called the incident suspicious and a precooked recipe for war. He said Tehran would work with Kuwait and Italy to determine the cause.

Why it matters. The BBC reports the strike came just over a week after Kuwait said it repelled Iranian drone attacks on an airbase hosting U.S. forces. Speaking on PBS late Thursday, Trump said victory in the Iran conflict would come whenever he wants, CNBC reports.

Zelensky Warns Russia Is Preparing a Massive Strike on the Grid

Moscow is completing preparations for a new massive strike on Ukraine's energy system, President Volodymyr Zelensky said Thursday, the AP reports via ABC News. He cited Ukrainian intelligence.

Zelensky said Russians continue to target substations, overhead power lines and the entire grid, the AP reports. Kyiv says the strikes aim to deprive civilians of light, heating and water as winter approaches. Ukraine is pushing partners for more air defense supplies.

In a BBC interview, Zelensky said the U.S. was not pushing Russia hard enough in peace talks. He called it strange that Trump posted a photo of himself embracing Putin. He said delays in anti-ballistic missiles were costing Ukrainian lives.

Why it matters. Zelensky told the BBC that peace talks were expected to resume in December. U.S. envoy Jared Kushner said Putin had agreed not to use nuclear weapons, the AP reports. The AP reports talks have hit a little bit of a deadlock over territory.

New Zealand's Final Count Leaves Luxon Needing a Kingmaker

What happened

New Zealand's ruling coalition has fallen one seat short of a parliamentary majority, official results released Friday show, the BBC reports. It now needs the centrist Opportunity Party to stay in power. If no deal is reached, the country may have to vote again.

Why it matters

Prime Minister Christopher Luxon's National Party won 40 seats, Al Jazeera reports. Partners ACT and New Zealand First won 13 and 12. That gives the bloc 65 seats in the 130-member parliament. Labour won 36 seats, and Opportunity won seven. The BBC reports a Labour-led government is not possible even with Opportunity.

What each side says

The BBC reports the coalition lost two seats on special votes cast by people overseas or away from their electorate. Those votes typically skew left. Al Jazeera reports Luxon's loss of support was driven by a stagnant economy. Many voters told the BBC of frustration over living costs and high unemployment.

What comes next

Luxon said negotiations may be challenging and will take time, the BBC reports. Opportunity leader Qiulae Wong said her party will negotiate with National, but all options remain open. Al Jazeera reports she has promised a universal basic income of up to NZ$370 a week. Failure to agree could send voters back to the polls next year, Al Jazeera reports.
