A Fed Governor Backs a December Rate Hike Before Inflation Data

Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller said Tuesday that he would support a quarter point rate increase in December. CNBC reports that he spoke in London and said a half point move should be considered if inflation runs hotter than expected.

Waller argued that Fed messaging around the October meeting led markets to expect more increases. He warned that failing to deliver them would put the Fed’s credibility at risk. The Fed held rates in October, with two officials dissenting.

His remarks came hours before the October consumer price index release. Bloomberg reports that economists expect prices to rise 0.3% for the month, after a 0.6% increase previously. Yahoo Finance reports that stock futures were little changed after Monday’s rally.

Why it matters. Higher rates raise borrowing costs for households and businesses. Bloomberg reports that two year yields rose as traders moved to fully price in a December increase. The Fed’s next policy meeting is scheduled for Dec. 8 and 9, according to CNBC.

The Supreme Court Lets Big Bend Border Wall Construction Resume

The Supreme Court on Monday allowed the Trump administration to resume border wall construction in the Big Bend region of West Texas. SCOTUSblog reports that the unsigned order paused a district judge’s ruling that had halted the project.

Only Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson indicated that she would have denied the request, without explaining why. The Texas Tribune reports that the order holds while the administration asks the court to review the case.

Judge Kathleen Cardone had found that challengers were likely to win their argument against waivers of environmental laws. SCOTUSblog reports that she noted the region accounts for a small share of border apprehensions. The administration argued that Congress clearly authorized the waivers.

Why it matters. The region includes parts of Big Bend National Park. Challengers warned that construction would bring blasting and bulldozing to a remote landscape. The court said the government’s interest outweighs the alleged environmental harm. The underlying case is not yet resolved.

France Blames Iran’s Revolutionary Guards for Supermarket Freezer Hacks

France on Monday blamed Iran’s Revolutionary Guards for cyberattacks that disrupted supermarket refrigerators and freezers last month. The Guardian reports that the foreign minister called the attack unprecedented and promised sanctions across Europe.

Hackers overrode thermostats in as many as 700 stores in October, pushing some temperatures above 50C. The Guardian reports that some stores discarded entire inventories of perishable food. France also summoned Iran’s ambassador.

Paris prosecutor Laure Beccuau said investigators identified methods linked to the Iranian group Shahid Kaveh. The Guardian reports that the evidence cited included Farsi and Iranian IP addresses. TechCrunch reports that blood banks were also hacked, but those attacks were stopped before any blood spoiled.

Why it matters. The attribution turns a retail disruption into a security dispute between Europe and Iran. TechCrunch reports that some affected controllers no longer receive security fixes. Iran’s consulate in New York did not immediately respond to TechCrunch’s request for comment.

Ukraine, Russia and the United States Open Talks in Abu Dhabi

Negotiators from Ukraine, Russia and the United States began talks in Abu Dhabi on Tuesday aimed at ending the war. Interfax Ukraine reports that the delegations moved to a trilateral format, according to Ukrainian negotiator Rustem Umerov.

Hours earlier, Russia attacked 10 Ukrainian regions overnight. The Guardian reports that strikes hit five major energy substations, citing Ukraine’s prime minister. Citing the Kyiv Independent, it reported at least nine deaths over the past day.

US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner joined the negotiations. Interfax Ukraine reports, citing Russian media, that Kushner expected two productive days of talks. He said the talks could create a new shared security system.

Why it matters. Reuters reports that these are the first talks since February aimed at ending the war. The Guardian reports that Ukraine’s president accused Russia of intensifying strikes to pressure Kyiv during the talks. No agreement has been announced.

03 / Deep dive

One story, explained.

What Trump’s Talks With Qatar Mean for Ending the Iran War

Part 1

President Trump said Monday there was positive momentum toward ending the war with Iran after meeting Qatar’s prime minister at the White House. Arab News, citing AFP and AP, reports that he called the war militarily effectively over. He also said a resumption of US strikes was quite unlikely.

Part 2

Qatar is leading mediation efforts, and its prime minister described positive discussions with Trump and Vice President JD Vance. Arab News reports that he said they discussed ways to progress the US and Iran talks. Al Monitor reports that Trump still described the United States as locked and loaded for renewed combat if needed.

Part 3

Major gaps remain between the two sides. Al Monitor reports that Secretary of State Marco Rubio called some Iranian demands outrageous and ridiculous. Al Monitor reports that Iran ties talks to seven conditions, including frozen assets and sanctions relief. Iranian security chief Mohsen Rezaei dismissed reports of talks, Arab News reports.

Part 4

Why it matters. Al Monitor reports that a June memorandum unraveled amid disputes over shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. Reuters reports that Trump plans to keep the naval blockade in the Strait of Hormuz for now. Bloomberg reports that Brent crude fell below $90 a barrel on hopes for peace.
