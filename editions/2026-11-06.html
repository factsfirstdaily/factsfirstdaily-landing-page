Raman Takes Los Angeles With Housing on Her Agenda

Nithya Raman defeated incumbent Karen Bass in the Los Angeles mayoral race after campaigning on housing, homelessness and basic city services. Bass conceded Thursday, saying the city needed everyone to help move it forward.

Raman, a city council member and Indian immigrant, promised more housing and fewer people living without shelter. Her platform also included protecting residents from immigration enforcement, preserving Hollywood jobs, repairing roads and improving neighborhood appearance.

The 2025 fires were a central issue in the race. Bass faced criticism over her absence during the emergency and the city response, the BBC reported.

Why it matters: Raman will lead a city preparing to host the 2027 Super Bowl and the 2028 Olympics. Her campaign commitments include both everyday services and the city housing crisis.

Planning Approval Does Not Clear the Trump Arch

A federal judge blocked construction of President Trump’s proposed arch for 14 days after a planning commission approved it. Judge Tanya Chutkan granted a request from Washington, D.C., and Virginia, which challenged the project and its expected disruptions.

The National Capital Planning Commission approved the project by eight votes to four following hours of public testimony. The proposed structure would rise 250 feet at Memorial Circle, between the Lincoln Memorial and Arlington National Cemetery.

The administration had said excavation could begin the same day as approval. Opponents argue the arch requires congressional authorization, which the administration has not sought, according to NPR.

Why it matters: Commission approval did not remove the court challenge or permit immediate construction. The order temporarily stops work; it is not a final decision on whether the arch is lawful.

Family Wealth Divides the First Home Market

A record share of Americans buying their first home paid entirely in cash, NPR reports, citing the National Association of Realtors. One in 10 made cash purchases, the highest share since those records began in 2003.

First time buyers accounted for only 22% of buyers in the report. Their median age remained 40, compared with 38 among those using mortgages and 54 among those paying cash.

Among first time cash buyers, 28% used an inheritance and 21% used proceeds from inherited property. Those figures describe cash buyers, not everyone purchasing a first home.

Why it matters: The report identifies a divide between people saving independently and those receiving family wealth. Personal savings still provided the biggest source of down payment funding for first time buyers overall.

Pope Leo’s Route Begins With Secular Uruguay

Pope Leo XIV departed Rome Friday for Uruguay, Argentina and Peru, beginning a Latin America trip scheduled through Nov. 17. Montevideo is his first destination, with arrival expected later Friday.

The itinerary opens in Uruguay, which AP describes as Latin America’s most secular country. His planned stops include a neighborhood affected by gang violence and Masses in Paysandú and Florida, outside the capital.

Argentina follows Uruguay before the pope travels to Peru. Leo served there as a missionary priest and bishop for two decades, giving the final stop a personal connection.

Why it matters: The journey combines a return to familiar territory with outreach in a country where Catholic practice is limited. It also comes as the 71 year old pope recovers from lung cancer surgery on Oct. 13.

Kenya’s 200 Year Sentence Is Not the Final Proceeding

What happened

A Kenyan court sentenced Paul Mackenzie to 200 years in prison for murdering 191 people, all but one children. The defendants had pleaded not guilty before their conviction on Oct. 19. Prosecutors sought execution, but Justice Diana Mochache declined to impose the death penalty.

The sentence

The judge imposed 100 years for homicide and another 100 for offenses related to starvation. Sentences within each category run together, while the two categories run consecutively. That structure produces the 200 year total rather than a single sentence for each victim.

The investigation

Mackenzie was arrested in 2023 after more than 400 bodies were discovered in Shakahola forest near Kenya’s coast. This murder case covered 191 victims, not every body found during the wider investigation. He was tried alongside 28 others, including guards and gravediggers, who received varying prison terms of up to 160 years.

The evidence

The court found an organized system of instructions compelling followers to fast. Postmortem examinations identified starvation in most deaths, with other victims dying from suffocation, strangulation or head injuries. The cause could not be determined for some decomposed bodies.

What follows

What follows: Mackenzie also faces separate manslaughter and terrorism charges, which he denies. Those proceedings are distinct from the murder conviction and the sentence now imposed. Their outcomes remain unresolved in the BBC reporting.
