Researchers Say the US Has Lost Its Measles Elimination Status

The United States has lost its measles elimination status, according to researchers with direct knowledge of the decision. NPR reports the Pan American Health Organization concluded measles has reestablished regular transmission in the country. The researchers spoke to KFF Health News.

The U.S. had held the status since 2000. CBS News reports the CDC has tallied 5,412 cases so far in 2026. That is more than double last year's total of 2,242 cases.

The CDC counts at least six measles related deaths this year, and some states report more. CBS News reports Pennsylvania alone has reported more than 1,500 cases and seven deaths associated with the disease this year.

Why it matters. Measles can now spread in ongoing chains among unvaccinated people. CBS News reports PAHO says a vaccination rate of 95% or higher is the best protection. CBS News reports PAHO is set to hold a news conference on Tuesday.

Two Big Nvidia Investors Urge the Board to Keep Huang's Return on the Table

Two large Nvidia shareholders asked the chipmaker's board to take its time choosing a new chief executive. CNBC reports Altimeter Capital's Brad Gerstner and Atreides Management's Gavin Baker want the search to leave room for Jensen Huang's return.

Huang resigned Thursday after reports about personal relationships, amid an independent board investigation. The letter did not call for his immediate reinstatement. It said interim chief Colette Kress can capably lead the company.

The two firms disclosed about $2.13 billion of Nvidia stock combined as of June 30. CNBC reports Elon Musk said he signed a petition to reinstate Huang.

Why it matters. Nvidia sits at the center of the artificial intelligence boom. CNBC reports a fund manager says other chip suppliers could fill demand if buyers lose trust in Nvidia. Investor Dan Ives says chip demand still outstrips supply four to one.

SpaceX Catches the Upper Stage of Starship for the First Time

SpaceX caught the upper stage of its Starship rocket with mechanical arms on its launch tower Saturday. CNBC reports it was the first catch of the spacecraft returning from orbit. The catch happened at Starbase, Texas.

The ship orbited Earth 14 times on a 22 hour mission. The Guardian reports the craft descended into the clutch of the tower's arms. BBC News reports SpaceX called the landing picture perfect.

SpaceX diverted the booster on Friday, and it exploded before hitting the Gulf of Mexico. CNBC reports the company also released 26 new Starlink satellites, adding to a constellation of over 11,000.

Why it matters. A reusable Starship could cut launch costs and speed satellite deployment. NASA is counting on Starship to carry astronauts from lunar orbit to the Moon's surface. Flight 15 was the first try at catching both stages.

Zelenskyy Says Ukrainians Would Get a Say on Any Donbas for NATO Deal

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy says U.S. negotiators asked whether Ukraine would leave Donbas if it were admitted to NATO. CBS News reports he said it was the first time the question had been raised.

Zelenskyy said Kyiv would not surrender its territory. CBS News reports he said any arrangement would not recognize occupied land as Russian. He also rejected a commitment to neutrality.

He is discussing a security package with NATO and European Union guarantees, troops, weapons and financial support. CBS News reports he said he would ask Ukrainians whether to accept such an exchange. He said he has no right to decide alone.

Why it matters. Control of Donbas has been a recurring sticking point in peace talks. CBS News reports Russian President Vladimir Putin has demanded Ukraine pull its forces out of the region as a precondition for peace. U.S. envoy Jared Kushner said Washington is now facilitating direct engagement between Moscow and European partners.

One story, explained.

What Israel's Killing of a Hamas Commander Means for the Gaza Truce

Part 1

An Israeli airstrike killed the commander of Hamas's Rafah Brigade in southern Gaza on Saturday. BBC News reports Samir Abu Younis was killed in the Al-Mawasi area. Hamas confirmed his death.

Part 2

What happened. BBC News reports eyewitnesses said four missiles struck a tent in a camp sheltering hundreds of displaced people. Al Jazeera reports the strike wounded two others. Israel's military said it took steps to limit harm to civilians.

Part 3

The backstory. Hamas's armed wing called Younis one of the most important commanders in planning the Oct. 7, 2023 attack. He had led the brigade since Sept. 15. He took over after the death of the previous commander. Al Jazeera reports Israel had designated Al-Mawasi a humanitarian zone but has struck it repeatedly.

Part 4

What comes next. A ceasefire took effect on Oct. 11, 2025. Strikes have continued since then. Gaza's Health Ministry says Israeli attacks killed 16 people over 48 hours. It counts at least 1,643 Palestinians killed since the truce began. Cold related deaths have risen to 11, including a baby who died of hypothermia. Most residents, many of them displaced, live in tents or damaged buildings after recent heavy rains. Hamas said Younis's funeral would be held Sunday afternoon in Khan Younis.
