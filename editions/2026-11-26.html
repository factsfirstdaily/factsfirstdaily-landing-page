Storm Postpones the Macy's Parade for the First Time

The Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade has been postponed due to weather for the first time in its history, CBS News reports. The 100th parade will now start Saturday at 8:30 a.m.

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani pointed to a heavy storm expected Thursday, CBS reports. He said wind gusts up to 65 mph were possible, along with potential flash flooding. Macy's said safety was its top priority.

Gov. Kathy Hochul declared a state of emergency for parts of southern New York, ABC News reports. Her office warned of damaging winds, heavy rain, travel disruptions and power outages. More than 80 million people from Georgia to Massachusetts are under wind alerts.

Why it matters. Some 81.5 million people are expected to travel between Thursday and Monday, according to AAA figures cited by NPR. NPR reports hundreds of flights had been canceled or delayed by early Thursday. High tides have also flooded streets along California's shorelines.

Judge Blocks the FCC's Early Review of ABC's Licenses

A federal judge on Wednesday blocked the FCC from moving forward with an early review of ABC's broadcast licenses, CBS News reports. U.S. District Judge Loren AliKhan issued a preliminary injunction.

AliKhan found the early reviews represented retaliation for speech the Trump administration resented, NPR reports. NPR says the cited offenses included Jimmy Kimmel's jokes, remarks on The View and ABC's moderation of a 2024 presidential debate.

The judge pointed to at least 11 examples of President Trump urging the FCC to strip ABC's licenses, CBS reports. She did not grant ABC's request for a broader ban. An FCC spokesperson called the ruling wrong on the facts and the law.

Why it matters. The order halts the early-renewal process for Disney-owned ABC stations for now, CNBC reports. NPR reports ABC and Disney serve six of the 10 largest U.S. markets. The network's lawsuit against the agency continues.

FCC Clears SpaceX to Launch a Million Data Satellites

The FCC on Wednesday cleared SpaceX to deploy 1 million satellites for data centers in space, the AP reports via NPR.

The BBC calls it the first approval of its kind. SpaceX wants sunlight to power satellites that process data for artificial intelligence. The project is known as Starmind. FCC Chairman Brendan Carr said it opens a bold new frontier.

Under the license, deployment must begin no later than November 2033, the BBC reports. SpaceX must deploy 10% by November 2035 and 50% by November 2038. Musk welcomed the decision on social media.

Why it matters. Scientists and industry experts cite enormous technical and financial obstacles, NPR reports. Astronomers said the move would significantly undermine the public's ability to observe the night sky, the BBC reports. Rival Amazon called it a lofty ambition rather than a real plan.

Explosion at a Bangkok Oil Refinery Injures at Least Seven

Seven people were injured, four of them critically, after an explosion at an oil refinery in Bangkok, the BBC reports. Local government officials gave the figures. A fire broke out after the blast on Thursday.

The Phra Khanong facility is run by energy company Bangchak Corporation. Authorities urged residents in the surrounding area to evacuate, Al Jazeera reports. Thick black smoke swept across the Thai capital's skyline.

The company said the fire was brought under control and put out within 30 minutes, the BBC reports. Bangchak said it shut down production at the site. The cause of the blast is under investigation.

Why it matters. The refinery is the fifth biggest in Thailand and the only one in an urban area, the AP reports via ABC News. It has a listed capacity of 120,000 barrels a day. An explosion and fire hit the same refinery in July 2012.

One story, explained.

Fighting Hampers Congo's Ebola Response as Cases Pass 12,000

What happened

Confirmed Ebola cases in the Democratic Republic of the Congo have passed 12,000, government data shows, Al Jazeera reports. At least 12,021 infections have been confirmed, including 5,904 deaths. It is the country's deadliest outbreak on record.

Why it matters

The outbreak was declared in mid-May in Ituri province, Al Jazeera reports. It is caused by the rare Bundibugyo species of the virus, which has no approved treatments or vaccines. Cases have been reported in eight of the country's 26 provinces. Delayed detection, conflict and strained health services have accelerated the spread.

What each side says

Fighting in the east is now disrupting the response. The UN mission said clashes in Ituri between the army and Convention for the Popular Revolution (CRP) fighters have intensified. The CRP declared a unilateral ceasefire in May, but fighting has continued. A WHO official said about a dozen people were evacuated after three days trapped in a hospital during intense crossfire. A humanitarian worker told the AP her team came under gunfire on Monday while returning to Bunia.

What comes next

The CRP is led by Thomas Lubanga, whom the International Criminal Court convicted in 2012 of conscripting child soldiers. On Thursday, a Kenyan court ruled that a U.S.-backed Ebola quarantine site breached the constitution, Al Jazeera reports. The court ordered further works there stopped until the government meets its conditions. Kenyans have staged angry protests against the facility.
