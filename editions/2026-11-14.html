Fed's Goolsbee Says Inflation Is Moving the Wrong Way as Hike Bets Firm

Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee said Friday the central bank has work to do to restore its credibility on inflation. He told CNBC inflation is rising and starting to spread. He spoke after new wholesale price data.

The producer price index rose 0.3% in October, slightly below forecasts. CNBC reports it was up 6.5% from a year earlier, the biggest annual gain since November 2022. The 10-year Treasury yield rose to about 5.14%.

Goolsbee pointed to demand from the AI data center buildout as a new source of pressure. He said the Fed is not on a path back to its 2% target. He is not a voter on rates this year.

Why it matters. Higher rates raise costs for mortgages, car loans and credit cards. Traders now price a 100% chance of a hike at the Dec. 9 meeting, according to CME Group's FedWatch. CNBC puts the odds of a half-point move at about 14%.

Duffy Seeks $50 Billion After Aging Air Traffic Gear Halts US Flights

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy wants about $50 billion to overhaul air traffic control after old equipment forced a nationwide halt to departures Friday. BBC News reports it was the longest nationwide ground stop since the Sept. 11 attacks. It lasted more than four hours.

Duffy said airspace reopened at 8:15 p.m. ET Friday. CNBC reports more than 3,000 U.S. flights were canceled Saturday as of 5:45 a.m. ET, citing FlightAware. More than 3,500 planes were in the air when the order came.

Duffy said overloaded routers in an old system gave inconsistent data, so officials shut it down. Al Jazeera reports officials said new technology added to the system overloaded older parts. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said there was no sign of a cyberattack.

Why it matters. Duffy warned the old system is going to continue to fail. Al Jazeera reports he expects operations to be back in time for the Thanksgiving travel rush. Duffy said the effects could ripple for a few days.

Bessent Says Agencies Sought Curbs on a Firm Tied to the Nvidia Scandal

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Friday that several agencies wanted export curbs on Megaspeed for months. He told CNBC the Commerce Department is in charge of that decision. Megaspeed is at the center of the scandal that ended Jensen Huang's run as Nvidia chief.

Bessent said Treasury, Defense, State and the trade representative's office all backed adding Megaspeed to the entity list. He said leakage of advanced chips to China hurts U.S. companies. Commerce had earlier denied a reported clash between Bessent and Secretary Howard Lutnick.

Bessent said the real question is whether Nvidia knew its client. NBC News reports a senior U.S. official said the Nvidia case should have no bearing on Trump's coming China trip. The official said export controls are not up for negotiation.

Why it matters. The dispute exposes a rift inside the administration over chip policy. Commerce said Lutnick has agreed to appear voluntarily before the Senate Banking Committee. Commerce did not respond to CNBC about Bessent's remarks.

Qatar and Turkiye Sign a Mutual Defense Pact as Gulf Tensions Persist

Qatar and Turkiye have agreed to a mutual defense pact against any outside armed attack on either country. Al Jazeera reports it followed a meeting in Istanbul on Friday. Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdogan and Qatar's emir chaired the talks.

The two countries will consult on threats and expand joint drills, arms production and training. The joint communique did not say what would trigger the pact or how they would respond. An analyst at Qatar University said it is meant to deter, not to target a country.

The deal comes as the region deals with fallout from the U.S. and Israeli war on Iran. Al Jazeera's live coverage reports it also comes amid disruption to shipping in the Strait of Hormuz. Al Jazeera reports the communique also welcomed a defense memorandum signed by Turkiye, Qatar, Pakistan and Indonesia.

Why it matters. Gulf states are seeking new security ties and less reliance on outside powers. Ankara and Doha called for the swift and full reopening of safe passage through the Strait of Hormuz.

One story, explained.

What to Expect When Trump, Xi and Putin Meet in Shenzhen

Part 1

President Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin plan a first three-way meeting next week. NBC News reports it will take place on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen. The White House announced it Friday night.

Part 2

What happened. A White House spokesperson said Trump will discuss reducing nuclear weapons. A senior U.S. official said no agreement is expected and called it a first conversation. Trump also plans to raise the Iran war. A senior official said Ukraine is on a separate track and will not be discussed.

Part 3

The backstory. Al Jazeera reports the last U.S. and Russian nuclear arms treaty, New START, expired in February. Trump then sought a new deal that includes China. NBC News cites Pentagon estimates that China has more than 600 warheads. The U.S. and Russia each have more than 5,000.

Part 4

What comes next. The Guardian reports Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy is among those who fear a Yalta-style carve-up. Al Jazeera reports the Kremlin dismissed that comparison but called the meeting extremely significant. The Guardian reports China is already trying to push nuclear disarmament off the agenda. NBC News reports no separate one-on-one meeting with Putin is scheduled. Elon Musk will travel with Trump but skip the official meetings.
