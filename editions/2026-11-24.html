Pennsylvania Reports Its Ninth Measles Death This Year

Pennsylvania on Monday announced its ninth measles-related death this year. The patient was an unvaccinated Centre County resident, Al Jazeera reports, citing Reuters. State officials gave no further details about the patient.

Pennsylvania has the largest outbreak in the country, with over 1,600 cases, CBS News reports. The deaths are the state's first from measles in 35 years. Virginia reported a child's measles-related death three days earlier.

The CDC has confirmed 5,838 cases nationwide this year as of November 19, the highest since 1991. The CDC lists only six measles-associated deaths in 2026. Al Jazeera reports that gap stems from a change in federal data sources.

Why it matters. The U.S. lost its measles elimination status last week. Kindergarten vaccination rates have fallen below the 95% needed to protect vulnerable people, CBS reports. Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. said he continues to recommend the MMR vaccine.

Nvidia Brings Back Jensen Huang as CEO After Board Reversal

Nvidia's board said on Monday it had reinstated Jensen Huang as president and CEO. He had stepped down less than two weeks before, during a scandal over an affair, CBS News reports. U.S. intelligence agencies suspected the woman of spying for China, which China has denied.

The board said it received input from senior U.S. government officials and is confident his return poses no national security risk. Board chair Harvey Jones had said last week the company did not intend to reinstate him.

Employees cheered Huang as he returned to the office on Monday, CNBC reports. He told staff he would be a better CEO the second time around. President Trump said he had spoken to the board.

Why it matters. Nvidia stock soared more than 30% on the news, CBS reports. Its chips power much of the AI boom. The Commerce Department also said it plans to blacklist Megaspeed, a Singapore firm tied to the scandal, the BBC reports.

Rep. Jimmy Gomez Will Leave Congress Dec. 1 After Ethics Findings

Rep. Jimmy Gomez, a California Democrat, announced Monday that he is leaving Congress. A bipartisan House Ethics report found substantial evidence of sexual misconduct toward a House staffer, NPR reports. His resignation takes effect Dec. 1.

The committee said his conduct might amount to criminal misdemeanor sexual abuse, CBS News reports. No criminal charges have been filed, ABC News reports. The panel said it interviewed 10 witnesses, including Gomez.

Gomez denied wrongdoing and said the report left out key parts of his testimony, ABC reports. He said he strongly disagreed with its conclusions. Attorneys for the staffer praised the report.

Why it matters. Gomez had already lost his reelection race to fellow Democrat Angela Gonzales-Torres. The committee did not recommend a punishment, citing too little time, CBS reports. It said it published the report so the House is aware.

Malaysia to Send Thousands Back to Myanmar After Genocide Ruling

Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim said on Tuesday that Malaysia will return more than 6,500 Myanmar nationals to their home country. He told parliament 1,500 will return on three navy vessels on Dec. 6 and 7, Al Jazeera reports. Another 5,303 will fly back in December at Myanmar's expense.

The announcement came five days after the International Court of Justice found Myanmar guilty of genocide against its Rohingya minority. The BBC reports no state had ever before been convicted of the crime. Myanmar denied the claims.

It is unclear how many Rohingya are among those facing deportation, The Guardian reports. The UN says Rohingya make up two-thirds of the 190,000 Myanmar refugees and asylum seekers in Malaysia. Anwar said Malaysia can no longer afford to host refugees.

Why it matters. The court said returns must be safe, voluntary and dignified, Al Jazeera reports. Rights groups have raised concerns about earlier returns. The UK imposed sanctions on five top Myanmar military officers after the ruling, the BBC reports.

Why Blocked Sea Lanes Now Threaten Food Aid for Millions

What happened

World Food Programme chief Luke Lindberg warned the UN Security Council on Monday about a triple chokepoint. He named the Strait of Hormuz, the Bab al-Mandeb and the Black Sea, Al Jazeera reports. He said disruption there had dramatically tightened the supply of food exports and farming inputs. He also cited conflicts and climate shocks such as El Nino.

Why it matters

Hormuz remains dangerous. Iran's Revolutionary Guards said mines in the strait set three oil tankers ablaze, CNBC reported early Tuesday. The Guards said the ships were under U.S. escort. The U.S. announced new Iran sanctions on Monday.

What each side says

The WFP expects $727 million in added costs by mid-2027 if conditions persist, putting aid for 7.3 million people at risk. About 30% of global fertilizer supplies pass through Hormuz. By mid-October, 63% fewer wheat and maize vessels were moving through the Bosphorus than a year earlier, the WFP said.

What comes next

Lindberg said grain is available but is not reaching people who need it, Al Jazeera reports. Some 266 million people face acute hunger, with famine risk in Sudan, South Sudan and Somalia. In Sudan, the cost of a basic diet has risen 130% since February. Lindberg urged member states to commit to three red lines. They are no more famines, no more weaponization of food and no more attacks on humanitarians.
