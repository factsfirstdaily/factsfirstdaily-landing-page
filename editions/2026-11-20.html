Record Rains Flood Florida's Space Coast as California Storms Persist

Storms that set rain records in South Florida moved north to the Space Coast on Thursday. The Associated Press reports rivers overflowed and crews rescued people and horses. Heavy rain also kept hitting California.

First responders made nearly 100 high-water rescues in Brevard County by Thursday, Sheriff Wayne Ivey said. The St. Johns River near Cocoa rose above a record flood level set in 1953. Nearly 200 Florida National Guard personnel were deployed, state officials said.

In California, The Guardian reports an atmospheric river pounded the south a day after record rain in San Francisco. Drivers were rescued from flooded streets near the Burbank airport. More than 200 flights at Los Angeles International Airport were delayed Thursday, the AP reported.

Why it matters. The weather service said heavy rain and flash flooding could last through Friday and may reach the Carolinas. Forecasters expect El Nino conditions to persist through spring. That means more extreme weather is likely in both states this winter.

White House Cyber Director Leaves After a Week of Tech Outages

National cyber director Sean Cairncross is leaving the Trump administration. NBC News reports a White House official confirmed his exit on Thursday. It follows a series of tech outages that hit companies and government agencies.

BBC News reports CBS and AFP were told by sources that Cairncross was fired. A person close to the White House told Reuters the president fully supported the firing. Cairncross declined to comment, and the White House has not publicly given a reason.

Defense Department chief information officer Kirsten Davies will serve in an acting capacity, a source told NBC News. Secretary of State Marco Rubio announced her appointment on X, BBC News reports. The permanent job requires Senate confirmation. Democrats will control the Senate starting in January.

Why it matters. The outages hit air travel, banks and a National Security Agency network. The top U.S. intelligence official said they were not a coordinated or state-sponsored attack. Officials have said the outages do not appear to be cyberattacks, NBC News reports.

Daily Pill Shrinks Pancreatic Tumors in an Early Trial

An experimental daily pill shrank tumors in many patients with advanced pancreatic cancer in an early trial. BBC News reports experts call it the biggest breakthrough for the disease in decades. The drug is called daraxonrasib.

The pill was given with chemotherapy to 61 recently diagnosed patients whose cancer had spread. The Guardian reports tumors stopped growing in almost all of them. In one in 12 patients, the cancer became undetectable on scans.

Doctors stress the pill is not a cure. It is unclear how long it can hold the disease back. The Guardian reports 76.6% of patients were alive a year after starting treatment. Usually 40% to 45% on chemotherapy alone survive a year.

Why it matters. Pancreatic cancer is one of the deadliest and hardest cancers to treat. Most patients with advanced disease survive months, not years, the BBC reports. Longer studies are needed to see whether any remissions last.

Britain and the EU Hold a Brussels Summit on a Reset Deal

British Prime Minister Andy Burnham is meeting top European Union leaders in Brussels on Friday. The Associated Press reports it is only the second EU and U.K. summit since Brexit. A package of agreements is expected to be signed.

The Guardian reports negotiators provisionally agreed to let 100,000 EU citizens come to the U.K. each year. The plan is a new youth mobility scheme. EU sources suggested Thursday night that both sides were on track for a deal.

The EU dropped a last-minute demand on tuition fees for its students, the Guardian reported. Details will be worked out within six months. Food and farming talks went down to the wire.

Why it matters. Burnham said the Brexit Britain was given has not worked. He has said rejoining should be on the table but is far from imminent. Reform UK leader Nigel Farage called the approach a betrayal, the Guardian reported.

One story, explained.

Why Russia Struck the Bridges of Kyiv

What happened

Russia launched one of the largest air attacks of the war on Ukraine overnight into Friday. BBC News reports ballistic missiles hit three bridges over the Dnipro River in Kyiv. Eight people were killed as of Friday morning, the BBC reports. Prime Minister Serhiy Koretsky said 28 were wounded. Six of the dead were in the capital. Two people died in a strike on a railway station in Lviv.

Why it matters

The strikes followed a threat from Vladimir Putin. The Guardian reports Putin vowed a painful response to Ukraine bombing the Kerch bridge. That bridge links Russia to occupied Crimea. Ukraine's military calls it a main supply route for Russian forces. Russia said the Kyiv bridges were used to move weapons to the front, Al Jazeera reports.

What each side says

Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko urged people to avoid driving into the city unless necessary. The three bridges are closed to traffic, the BBC reported. Nuclear plants cut output, causing outages in Kyiv and 10 regions, a deputy energy minister said. Ukraine said it is still hitting Russian oil and defense sites.

What comes next

The attack came hours after European leaders said they were ready for talks with Moscow on ending the war. A Russian projectile reportedly landed inside Poland, and Polish jets were scrambled. U.S. envoy Jared Kushner said the U.S. wants a deal with Europe by its side. Putin has pledged to rebuild the Kerch bridge.
