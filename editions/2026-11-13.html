Van Hollen Launches a Bid to Unseat Schumer as Senate Democratic Leader

Sen. Chris Van Hollen of Maryland said Thursday he will run for Senate majority leader. NBC News reports he is the first to challenge Sen. Chuck Schumer for the post. Schumer has led Senate Democrats since 2017.

Van Hollen said voters called for change from the status quo. CNBC reports he claims support from more than 10 of the 55 Democratic senators. He called himself the underdog. Sen. Bernie Sanders and Sen.-elect Abdul El-Sayed of Michigan back him.

Schumer answered on X that he has the votes to stay on as leader. Democratic senators will choose their leaders in a closed-door vote in December. A simple majority of the caucus is needed to win.

Why it matters. The winner will run the new Democratic Senate majority. Van Hollen wants to use a coming government funding bill to push to end the Iran war. That measure is needed to avoid a December shutdown. Schumer remains favored to keep the job.

HUD Rule Would Bar Mixed-Status Families From Subsidized Housing

The Department of Housing and Urban Development issued a rule Thursday on families with mixed immigration status. NPR reports it bars those families from federally subsidized housing. Critics say tens of thousands of U.S. citizen children could be pushed out.

Undocumented immigrants do not get federal rental aid. Since the 1990s, Congress has let them live with relatives who do. HUD Secretary Scott Turner said the agency is closing a decades-old loophole.

The rule requires every household member to verify status through a federal data system called SAVE. The National Housing Law Project says the rule uses HUD for immigration enforcement. The group expects a legal challenge.

Why it matters. The rule takes effect in 30 days. Local housing authorities then get a few months to identify residents who must leave. HUD notes in the rule that removing these families will cost the government more. CBS News cites research estimating about 20,000 mixed-status families could be affected.

A Global Microsoft Outage Knocks Out Outlook and Teams for Hours

A global Microsoft outage disrupted Outlook, Teams and Copilot from Thursday into Friday. NBC News reports Microsoft was still working on the problem more than 12 hours later. The company said it saw no sign of malicious activity.

Problems began around midday Thursday in the U.S. Al Jazeera reports Downdetector logged complaints from 74 countries and territories within about an hour. Microsoft said a number of servers had become unexpectedly unavailable.

CBS News reports OpenAI and Anthropic also had trouble connecting with Microsoft 365 tools. Workhorse Group delayed an earnings call because of the outage. NBC News reports agencies in California and on Capitol Hill also had email problems.

Why it matters. About 450 million people use Microsoft email and workplace apps, NBC News reports. NBC News reports Microsoft said around 6 a.m. ET Friday that traffic was being gradually restored. It warned some users may still see intermittent issues. Microsoft promised a full report on the root cause once the problem is fixed.

Iran Says It Struck a US Radar Site in Bahrain After an Attack at Home

Iran's Revolutionary Guard says it targeted a U.S. early warning radar in Bahrain. Al Jazeera reports Iran called it retaliation for an air attack on a telecom tower in southern Iran. Washington and Manama had not commented when Al Jazeera reported the claim.

Iranian state media said the port city of Sirik was hit by enemy fire on Thursday. Reuters says it is Iran's first claimed attack on U.S. military assets in a Gulf country since early September. Bahrain hosts the U.S. Navy's Fifth Fleet headquarters.

The claim followed a shift by Trump on diplomacy. NBC News reports Trump wrote that the U.S. does not need a deal with Iran. He asked Vice President JD Vance to focus on other issues.

Why it matters. The war began Feb. 28 with U.S. and Israeli attacks on Iran. U.S. Central Command says it is still enforcing a naval blockade of Iranian ports. The Guard warned it is ready to engage without limits if attacks recur.

One story, explained.

How a Warming Climate and a Strong Drought Fueled Indonesia's Worst Fires in Years

Part 1

Scientists say Indonesia's fiercest fires in at least a decade had three main drivers. The Guardian reports World Weather Attribution blamed El Niño, global heating and forest loss. Fires have burned almost 3 million hectares this year.

Part 2

What happened. The team studied Java, Kalimantan and Sumatra during the peak season from August to October. It found global heating raised temperatures by 1.6C over that period. El Niño added another 0.3C and cut rainfall by about 50%. Imperial College London climate scientist Friederike Otto said El Niño turned off the taps, but fossil fuel emissions turned up the heat.

Part 3

The backstory. Hot, dry conditions were three times more likely because of climate change and five times more likely because of El Niño. Reuters reports a Melbourne University researcher said September 2026 was Indonesia's fifth-driest September since 1991. Decades of logging and peatland drainage also left land primed to burn. Local reports put the area burned so far in 2026 at 2.88 million hectares. That would top this century's record of 2.6 million, set in 2015.

Part 4

What comes next. The health ministry counts at least 400,000 related respiratory infections, including 180,000 children. Smog has reached Singapore, Malaysia and the Philippines. Protesters in Jakarta want the fires declared a national emergency. Fires still burn because El Niño delayed the rainy season by 20 to 30 days. A Red Cross climate adviser said El Niño's predictability allows preventive action before future seasons.
