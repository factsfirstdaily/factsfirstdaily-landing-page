ICE Arrests at Seattle and Reagan National Airports Draw Outcry

Masked immigration officers arrested two passengers on a jet bridge at Seattle-Tacoma International Airport on Saturday. CBS News reports the arrests delayed an Alaska Airlines flight to Los Angeles. Alaska Airlines said ICE gave no advance notice.

DHS said ICE targeted Ricardo Larrondo Mendoza, who it alleged overstayed his visa, ABC News reports. DHS alleged a second passenger, Sofia Hernandez Mejia, threw hot coffee on officers. DHS said she will be charged with assaulting a federal officer.

Washington Gov. Bob Ferguson called the video horrific and chilling. On Thursday, ICE detained Princeton Ph.D. student Eva Yi Xie at Reagan National Airport. Her attorney says her asylum application has been pending for 10 years, NBC News reports.

Why it matters. DHS said Xie never held valid authorization to study in the U.S., CBS News reports. Her attorney called the arrest without legal basis. CBS reports ICE has stepped up airport arrests since the summer.

Trump Says the FBI's National Crime Database Was Hacked

President Trump said on Saturday that the FBI's National Crime Information Center database was targeted in a hack. A law enforcement source confirmed the hack to ABC News. Trump spoke in a phone interview with Fox News.

"I will say that it's not good. I don't like that," Trump said. ABC News reports the scale of the intrusion and who is behind it remain unclear. The FBI declined to comment to ABC News.

Trump also suggested there could be a positive spin, CNBC reports. He said maybe we will learn about voter fraud. CNBC reports it is unclear what data was taken. The database is restricted to law enforcement but is not classified.

Why it matters. The BBC reports police use the database to share information on warrants and background checks. It would follow a September breach of an FBI jobs website. That hack was claimed by a group called ShinyHunters.

Fire at an Indonesian Factory Leaves at Least Three Dead and Dozens Missing

A fire that broke out Saturday at a confectionery factory west of Jakarta has killed at least three people. The BBC reported on Sunday that 36 people were still missing. By Sunday midday, rescuers had recovered six body bags and were searching for 33 people, BBC News Indonesia reports.

The factory is in Serang district, Banten province, about 50 kilometers west of Jakarta. Banten Governor Andra Soni said about 60 people were believed to be inside, the BBC reported. Indonesia's rescue agency said 15 survivors had been located.

Drone footage showed large parts of the factory destroyed, with the roof collapsed in places, the Guardian reports. Rescuers entered the charred building on Sunday to search for the missing. Small fires were still burning in some spots.

Why it matters. Relatives of the missing gathered near the factory entrance seeking updates, the Guardian reports. Authorities have not yet determined the cause of the fire. The search for dozens of workers continues.

Vance Declines to Back Trump's Iran Claim as Gold Star Families Seek Answers

Vice President JD Vance stopped short on Friday of endorsing President Trump's claim about Iran. Trump has said Iran was so close to a nuclear weapon that war was essential, the Guardian reports. Vance said it was not his place to second-guess the president.

Vance said his job is to support the president, according to the Guardian. On Friday, Trump said he would have added that he would do it again. After this month's midterms, Vance had posted that "the reason we lost is the war."

Gold Star families told CBS News that briefings on a Pentagon probe did not address their concerns to their satisfaction. The probe covers a March 1 drone strike in Kuwait. It killed six U.S. Army Reserve soldiers at a command post.

Why it matters. CBS News reports the Pentagon has indicated the report could be made public in a matter of days. Some families said they were frustrated by what they heard. A surviving soldier has alleged in a whistleblower complaint to Congress that officials ignored drone warnings.

One story, explained.

Why Mexico's Capture of El Chapo Isidro Matters

What happened

Mexican special forces captured Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, known as El Chapo Isidro, in Guasave, Sinaloa, on Friday night. He was on the FBI list of 10 most wanted fugitives, the BBC reports. Mexico's defense ministry said U.S. intelligence helped locate him. The U.S. had offered a $5 million reward for information leading to his arrest. He had evaded arrest for more than a decade, the Guardian reports.

Why it matters

The FBI alleges his group trafficked heroin, methamphetamine, cocaine, fentanyl and marijuana into the United States. Mexico also arrested his alleged second-in-command and an alleged financial operator, CBS News reports. Eight people were detained in total. The three men were flown to Mexico City to face prosecutors. The BBC reports the U.S. is seeking his extradition.

What each side says

The raid set off hours of violence in Guasave. Mexico said seven alleged cartel gunmen and four soldiers were killed, Al Jazeera reports. Videos showed circling helicopters and people fleeing gunfire at a baseball game, NPR reports. Roadblocks and burning trucks hit the city.

What comes next

The AP, via NPR, reports he was a key ally of Los Mayos, a faction of the Sinaloa Cartel. That faction is fighting a rival group for control of Sinaloa. Experts warned that removing cartel leaders could set off another wave of violence. President Trump has pressured Mexico to go after criminal groups.
