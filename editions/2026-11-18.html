The US Strikes an Iranian Island as Gulf Allies Fend Off Retaliation

The U.S. military struck a missile site on Iran's Qeshm Island on Tuesday, a U.S. military official said. CBS News reports the official called it a self-defense strike against Revolutionary Guard aggression. The island sits in the Strait of Hormuz.

The strike came hours after Iran said it targeted a U.S. radar system in Bahrain. Al Jazeera reports U.S. Central Command said the Iranian drones were intercepted without damage. An Iranian provincial official said the U.S. strikes caused no damage to residential or commercial areas.

Bahrain and Kuwait said Wednesday they intercepted weapons launched by Iran. Iran said its drones targeted Patriot air defense systems at a U.S.-used air base in Kuwait. The UAE's state oil company said a crew member was missing after its ship was struck in the strait.

Why it matters. The exchanges mark a new round of tit-for-tat strikes in the U.S.-Iran war. Al Jazeera reports a peace deal remains elusive as tensions escalate. Kuwait said the attacks threaten regional security and undermine diplomatic efforts to de-escalate.

Democrats Win in Alaska and Put Trump Allies on Notice

Democrat Mary Peltola has won Alaska's Senate race, defeating Republican incumbent Dan Sullivan. BBC News reports the win gives Democrats a 56th Senate seat. It took two full weeks of counting to call the Nov. 3 race.

Peltola is the first Alaska Native elected to the U.S. Senate. She is also the first Democrat to win a Senate seat in Alaska since 2008. Democrats also won back the House in the midterms.

On Wednesday, Rep. Robert Garcia sent a dozen letters telling Trump associates to save their records. NBC News reports recipients include Vice President JD Vance and Trump sons Donald Jr. and Eric. Garcia is expected to chair the Oversight Committee when Democrats take control early next year.

Why it matters. The letters preview the committee's priorities for the new Congress. They focus on allegations of corruption and the handling of the Jeffrey Epstein files. Garcia said Democrats want to end the administration's "self-dealing, misconduct, and abuse." He called the letters "just the beginning."

Rubio Says a Week of Tech Outages Was Not a Coordinated Attack

Secretary of State Marco Rubio said there is "zero evidence" linking a week of U.S. tech outages. BBC News reports he told reporters, "We are not under systemic attack". He spoke as President Donald Trump traveled in Asia.

The disruptions began Thursday with a Microsoft outage that hit email and other services. The next day, the FAA halted flights across the U.S. for more than four hours. Apple users lost iCloud and Apple Pay on Tuesday.

The Associated Press reports the outages also hit JPMorgan Chase and a National Security Agency network. Rubio said fixing a known vulnerability can create a short-term disruption. Every affected company or agency said its issue was not cyber-related, according to BBC News.

Why it matters. The outages left Americans worried about email, bank accounts and air travel. The AP reports they put the administration on the defensive as Trump tries to project strength abroad. Trump had said on Tuesday in Manila, "It's fine."

Britain Sanctions Eight Firms Over West Bank Settlement Building

Britain has sanctioned eight construction and property firms over Israeli settlement building in the occupied West Bank. BBC News reports it is the first package since the government announced a tougher approach. BBC News notes the settlements are illegal under international law.

BBC News reports the government said the sanctions would disrupt the firms' flows of finance. The sanctions also target Tzav 9, an Israeli group the UK says has blocked aid to Gaza. Tzav 9 said it has always protested peacefully.

Israeli Foreign Minister Gideon Saar called the sanctions a "hostile act." He said Israel will respond with countermeasures. The Palestinian foreign ministry welcomed the move and urged other countries to follow.

Why it matters. The sanctions land just before bid deadlines for construction tenders in the E1 area near Jerusalem. Al Jazeera reports the E1 project would split the West Bank and cut it off from East Jerusalem. Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband told companies, "do not bid, do not build".

03 / Deep dive

One story, explained.

Xi Greets Trump on the Tarmac Ahead of Talks With Putin

What happened

President Donald Trump landed in Shenzhen, China, on Wednesday for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit. NBC News reports Chinese leader Xi Jinping personally greeted him on the tarmac. It is believed to be the first time Xi has greeted a foreign leader on arrival. Xi was returning the gesture Trump made in September.

Why it matters

CNBC reports the three leaders are set to meet on Thursday for historic trilateral talks. Talks are expected to focus on the Iran war, Russia's war in Ukraine and strategic stability. Trump said he plans to discuss limiting nuclear weapons and ending the war in Ukraine. He said a Ukraine deal is "very close."

What each side says

Chinese officials said the leaders will hold in-depth talks on world peace and stability. Putin told the summit that sanctions on Russia have backfired. Al Jazeera reports a Crisis Group analyst said a Trump-Xi meeting is unlikely to produce substantive breakthroughs. Putin adviser Yury Ushakov called the meetings' symbolism hard to overestimate.

What comes next

Expectations for a concrete outcome are low. The AP reports analysts are not expecting breakthroughs. Trump said he does not plan to raise Taiwan but expects Xi to. The resignation of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang is also expected to loom over any Trump-Xi meeting. Trump has also floated a meeting with North Korea's Kim Jong Un.
