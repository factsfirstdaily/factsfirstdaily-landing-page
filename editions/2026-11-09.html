Congress Returns With Five Weeks to Address Funding and Defense

Congress returns to Washington Monday for its final legislative stretch before the new Congress takes office in January. ABC reports that lawmakers face a five week session, with government funding and the annual defense policy bill still unresolved.

Federal funding expires after Dec. 11, leaving lawmakers to negotiate another spending measure to avoid a shutdown. CNBC reports that either a temporary extension or full appropriations legislation is needed. Senate rules make bipartisan support necessary.

The agenda also includes surveillance authority, the expired Farm Bill and presidential nominees. ABC reports that a procedural vote on permitting reform is scheduled Monday. Newly elected members are attending orientation while current lawmakers resume work.

Why it matters. The approaching deadline belongs to the current Congress, not the incoming majority. CNBC describes limited incentives for sweeping agreements. Funding and defense decisions cannot simply wait for January without consequences for government operations.

Nvidia Opens an Intelligence Inquiry as China Rejects Spy Claims

Nvidia is investigating whether its chief executive, Jensen Huang, was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort. Reuters reports that the company announced the inquiry Sunday. The board says it has no reason to believe company secrets or U.S. national security were compromised.

China rejected the allegations Monday, calling the Chinese spy narrative unsubstantiated. Reuters reports that foreign ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun addressed the matter in Beijing. That denial does not resolve the questions Nvidia has asked independent directors to examine.

The investigation follows reporting about Huang’s relationship with a Chinese businesswoman. Reuters reports that outside independent counsel will assist the board committee. Huang acknowledged being unfaithful to his wife and said he would cooperate with the review.

Why it matters. Nvidia supplies chips central to artificial intelligence development, making security and leadership questions consequential. Reuters reports that the board retains confidence in Huang and his leadership team. He remains in charge while the inquiry proceeds.

Egypt Investigates a Train Collision That Killed at Least Seven

Egyptian authorities are investigating a collision between two passenger trains south of Cairo on Sunday. AFP reports seven deaths and more than 100 injuries in Sunday’s crash. Several carriages derailed after one train struck another from behind.

The railway authority said a service traveling from Giza to Fayoum hit a train heading from Cairo to Aswan. The BBC reports that the collision occurred in Giza province. Emergency crews and cranes worked to reach passengers and clear the tracks.

President Abdel Fattah el Sisi ordered an urgent investigation and accountability for any negligence. AFP reports that prosecutors are examining the technical causes. The available reporting does not establish who or what caused the collision.

Why it matters. The crash disrupted rail services while hospitals treated scores of passengers. The BBC reports that hospitals were put on heightened alert. Trains between Giza and Upper Egypt were suspended while cranes were dispatched to remove the wreckage.

COP31 Puts Electrification and Climate Finance on the Negotiating Table

The COP31 climate summit opens Monday in Antalya, Turkey, with an electrification proposal at the center of the host’s agenda. Al Jazeera reports on the voluntary pledge, which seeks a larger role for electricity in global energy consumption.

The proposal targets an electricity share of at least 35% by 2035, compared with about one fifth today. Al Jazeera reports that climate advocates question its safeguards. They warn that more electricity does not automatically mean less reliance on fossil fuels.

Climate finance is another early source of disagreement as delegations gather for two weeks of negotiations. The Guardian reports tensions over a climate finance work programme. Developing countries want funding commitments to receive greater attention during the talks.

Why it matters. Electrification can change transport, industry and heating, but its climate benefits depend on how power is generated. Al Jazeera describes competing demands over fossil fuels. The summit must turn broad energy ambitions into practical commitments.

How Frozen Savings Turned Paraguay’s Bank Failure Into a Political Crisis

Part 1

Paraguay’s digital bank ueno has collapsed, temporarily locking nearly 3 million customers out of their savings in a country of 6.5 million. Reuters reports that the crisis unfolded over five days. The bank had expanded access to accounts for people underserved by traditional institutions, making its failure a nationwide household emergency.

Part 2

The central bank placed ueno into resolution Tuesday after finding that it had breached minimum solvency requirements. Reuters reports that leaked regulatory warnings had raised questions about concentrated lending. Public institutions, including the state pension fund, held roughly $850 million at the bank as of June, widening the potential consequences.

Part 3

Santiago Peña was impeached Friday, and Vice President Pedro Alliana replaced him as president after the banking crisis described by Reuters. Reuters reports that prosecutors charged Peña Saturday with breach of trust and administration for personal gain. He denies wrongdoing, and his lawyer says he is pleading not guilty to the charges.

Part 4

Resolution is not the same as immediate liquidation, and the central bank has begun returning money through deposit guarantees. Reuters reports that about $160 million had reached nearly 1.2 million savers by Saturday. That payout does not establish that every customer has recovered their savings or settle the criminal allegations.
