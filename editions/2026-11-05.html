The stories that matter most.

Democrats are projected to win control of both chambers of Congress

Democrats will control both the House and the Senate after Tuesday's midterm elections, according to projections by CBS News. The Associated Press called the Senate late Tuesday and the House early Wednesday, Forbes reports.

Democrats have won 54 Senate races and Republicans 44, with 2 still uncalled, Forbes reports. Democrats flipped seats in Ohio, North Carolina, Iowa, Kansas, Maine and South Carolina. CBS says the Kansas winner is the first Democrat to take a Senate seat there since 1932. Alaska and Mississippi were still too close to call, according to CBS.

President Trump congratulated Democrats in an interview with CBS and said he would still use his veto. Senate Majority Leader John Thune noted that Republicans still hold the White House.

When the new Congress meets in January, Republicans will hold the White House while Democrats lead both chambers. House Democrats are likely to back Hakeem Jeffries for Speaker, Time says. He would be the first Black Speaker in U.S. history.

Iran says four more tankers hit mines near the Strait of Hormuz

Iran's Revolutionary Guard says 4 oil tankers were destroyed after hitting naval mines south of the Strait of Hormuz, Reuters reported via Dawn. The Guard said the ships were still burning and that it had seized 2 other vessels.

The claims have not been independently verified, and Iran has made similar claims before, Gulf News notes. Days earlier, the Guard said 2 tankers exploded after hitting mines near Hormuz.

President Trump warned on Nov. 4 of a possible larger strike on Iran. Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei said anyone expecting a policy shift after the midterms was "seriously mistaken," Gulf News reports.

Indonesia's president fires his chief of staff over alleged wiretapping

Indonesia's president, Prabowo Subianto, fired chief of staff Listyo Sigit Prabowo on Nov. 4, a day after his arrest, Al Jazeera reports.

He is accused of illegally tapping the phones of the president, ministers and dozens of others, ABC News Australia reports. Two other senior police officers were also detained, and police seized 11 phones.

The president's spokesperson said the conduct may count as an act "tending towards treason." A senior minister said investigators had found no sign of treason. More than 20,000 soldiers were deployed in Jakarta the same day, which the military called a normal occurrence.

Spain's transport minister resigns after a train crash that killed 69

Spain's transport minister, Óscar Puente, has resigned six days after a high speed train crash near Urda killed 69 people, the BBC reports.

He told Congress that a transport minister cannot stay in office after two accidents this serious in less than a year. A crash on the same line in January killed 46 people.

Days earlier, Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez had rejected Puente's offer to step down, while opposition parties demanded his resignation.

03 / Deep dive

One story, explained.

Ethiopia expels Sudan's diplomats as fears of a wider war grow

What happened

Ethiopia has ordered 6 staff at Sudan's consulate in the border town of Assosa to leave within 48 hours, Reuters reports via ThePrint. A senior Ethiopian official confirmed the order on Nov. 4 but gave no reason. The move is the latest sign of falling relations between two neighbors that are each fighting their own wars.

Why it matters

Sudan's civil war began in April 2023 and has displaced more than 14 million people, according to the UN. It is one of the world's worst humanitarian crises. Ethiopia faces its own fighting in Tigray, where rebels launched an offensive in September. Each side's claims have raised fears that the two wars could spread into a wider conflict in the Horn of Africa, Reuters reports.

What each side says

Sudan accuses Ethiopia of backing the Rapid Support Forces, the paramilitary group fighting Sudan's army. On Nov. 1 it complained to the UN Security Council and the African Union that drones were launched from Ethiopian soil. Ethiopia denies this and accuses Sudan, Egypt and Eritrea of backing Tigrayan rebels. All three deny it.

What comes next

Ethiopia's prime minister's office did not immediately respond to Reuters. Amjad Farid, an adviser to Sudan's ruling council, said Sudan remains committed to defending its territory. He added that Sudan is making every possible effort to avoid an open war with Ethiopia. For now, the diplomats have 48 hours to go, and Sudan's complaint sits with the UN and the African Union.
