Nvidia's Board Weighs Whether to Keep Jensen Huang as CEO

Nvidia's board is weighing whether to keep Jensen Huang, the only CEO the chipmaker has ever had. Reuters, via RTE, reports that directors met after reports of two extramarital affairs. Huang acknowledged being unfaithful in a staff memo but gave no details.

The Guardian reports that Nvidia shares fell about 8% after the bell on Wednesday. The drop followed a Wall Street Journal report that the board was preparing to oust Huang. No departure was confirmed in reporting checked Thursday morning.

Bloomberg reports that the board is working with an outside firm to identify potential replacements. It is also weighing a temporary chief while it searches for a permanent one. Nvidia did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

Why it matters. Nvidia supplies chips at the center of the global AI boom. Reuters reports that Huang has no obvious successor and owns about 3.6% of Nvidia's shares. The company is due to report quarterly results on Nov. 17.

Treasury Yields Climb Past 5% as Bets on Faster Fed Hikes Build

U.S. bond trading resumed Thursday after the Veterans Day closure, and yields rose. CNBC reported the 10-year Treasury yield at about 5.01% in its 2:38 a.m. ET report. That benchmark helps set borrowing costs across the economy.

The move extends a sell-off that began after Tuesday's inflation report. Reuters reports that core consumer prices rose 0.3% in October for a third straight month. Before that release, Fed Governor Christopher Waller said a reading that high would warrant considering a half-point December hike.

CNBC reports that the dollar touched its highest level in about 19 months. Some brokerages, including Goldman Sachs, now expect another hike early next year. New York Fed President John Williams is due to speak late Thursday.

Why it matters. Higher yields raise borrowing costs for households and companies. Dallas Fed President Lorie Logan said the current 3.75% to 4.00% policy rate is too low. Producer price data is due Friday, ahead of the Fed's Dec. 8-9 meeting.

Eight Dead After a Brooklyn Building Collapses Into a Construction Site

A three-story building in Flatbush, Brooklyn, partly collapsed on Wednesday afternoon. ABC7 reported eight people dead and 17 hurt as of 7:36 a.m. ET Thursday. Seven children were among those treated, and at least three firefighters were injured.

The building at 1321 Flatbush Ave. had a store and apartments. It fell into an open construction site next door. Officials say others may still be missing, and rescue and recovery work continues.

CBS New York reports that workers were doing excavation at the neighboring lot when the collapse occurred. The city cited a demolition contractor there in April for ignoring a stop-work order. That violation is listed as resolved.

Why it matters. The cause is not yet known. CBS New York reports that the Department of Buildings is still investigating. Inspectors are checking nearby buildings while crews search the rubble.

U.S. Envoys Reach the Kremlin for Talks With Putin

U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner arrived at the Kremlin on Thursday for talks with President Vladimir Putin. Reuters, via ThePrint, reports this is Witkoff's 10th in-person meeting with Putin. The talks are part of a renewed push for a Ukraine peace deal.

The trip follows two days of U.S.-led talks with Ukraine and Russia in Abu Dhabi. Reuters reports the sides agreed on a major prisoner exchange in the coming weeks. They announced no breakthrough in the wider negotiations.

BBC News reports the envoys are expected to fly to Dublin to meet Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky. Kushner said they would ask Putin to back European participation in future talks. Russia has opposed the idea, citing European support for Kyiv.

Why it matters. Territory remains a central obstacle. Reuters reports Kyiv rejects Putin's demand to surrender the whole eastern Donbas region. Zelensky said Wednesday he hoped for a result by year end, but much depends on how Washington approaches Moscow.

Why Reported Israeli Preparations for an Iran Strike Raise the Stakes

Part 1

Israel's security cabinet reportedly ordered the military to step up preparations for a possible attack on Iran. Al Jazeera reports that public broadcaster KAN said the directives could lead to activities that trigger a war. The cabinet is chaired by caretaker Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. He has said the war on Iran will continue until the government in Tehran collapses.

Part 2

The timing is politically charged. Al Jazeera reports that Netanyahu's coalition lost its parliamentary majority in October's elections. Gadi Eisenkot has been offered the chance to form a government. He urged Netanyahu not to deploy the military without a direct and immediate threat before a new government is formed.

Part 3

Iran answered with a warning. Security chief Mohsen Rezaei said every base involved in a new U.S. attack, from West Asia to Europe, would be a target. Tehran also rejected a U.S. proposal to send its enriched uranium to a third country. Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf said the ball is in the American court.

Part 4

Why it matters. Washington is trying to keep diplomacy alive. Al Jazeera reports that Secretary of State Marco Rubio says economic pressure makes major combat operations unnecessary. Trump says he is prepared to resume the war if a Qatar-led diplomatic track fails. A possible Israeli strike adds uncertainty to those efforts.
