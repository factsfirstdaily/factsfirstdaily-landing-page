The House Shelves the Bid to Impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth

The House on Tuesday blocked an effort to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, at least for now. The BBC reports that lawmakers voted 211-204 to refer the articles to the Judiciary Committee. That step effectively kills the measure in this Congress.

Rep. Thomas Massie, a Kentucky Republican, filed eight articles accusing Hegseth of waging war on Iran without congressional authorization. Massie was the only Republican voting against sending the resolution to committee. Hegseth called the effort a farce at a Pentagon briefing.

NBC News reports that President Trump said the impeachment attempt had miserably failed. He praised Hegseth on Truth Social. House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries said the fight was just beginning.

Why it matters. Democrats take control of both chambers in January. Time reports that they could revive the effort when the new Congress convenes on Jan. 3. A House impeachment needs only a simple majority. Removal would still require two thirds of the Senate.

Jim Jordan Beats Steve Scalise to Lead House Republicans in the Minority

House Republicans on Tuesday chose Rep. Jim Jordan of Ohio as their leader for the new Congress. WBAL reports that he beat Rep. Steve Scalise of Louisiana 106 to 73. Speaker Mike Johnson had said he would step down from leadership.

Jordan argued that chairing the Judiciary Committee prepared him to fight Democratic investigations of the Trump administration. He also co-founded the House Freedom Caucus. Senate Republicans re-elected John Thune, who ran unopposed, as their leader.

Democrats face their own leadership fight. Roll Call reports that Sen. Bernie Sanders said he will not support Chuck Schumer for majority leader. Sen. Elissa Slotkin demanded a concrete plan for change. Schumer said he was confident he would be elected.

Why it matters. Both parties are resetting after last week’s midterm results. WBAL reports that party leaders set the tone on Capitol Hill and shape the policy playbook. Democrats will hold majorities in both chambers starting in January.

Ukraine and Russia Agree to a Prisoner Swap as Abu Dhabi Talks End

Ukraine, Russia and the United States concluded two days of talks in Abu Dhabi on Wednesday. The Guardian reports that Russian negotiator Vladimir Medinsky said Russia and Ukraine agreed to a large-scale prisoner swap. He said more talks would be held.

US envoy Jared Kushner called the discussions substantive but tough. He said he hoped to finalize the prisoner release during Thursday’s meeting with Putin. Kushner and Steve Witkoff plan to meet the Russian president in Moscow, then Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Dublin.

The sides remain divided over Europe’s role. Kushner said European partners need to be involved in discussions of Ukraine’s postwar security. Medinsky questioned what Russia and the European Union had to discuss.

Why it matters. The war has lasted nearly five years. Kushner said territorial issues would be the hardest to resolve. The prisoner-swap agreement is a humanitarian step, not a peace settlement; its final size and implementation remain unresolved.

Prosecutors Charge Three Men Over Alleged Iran-Backed Attacks and a UK Air Base Plot

Manhattan prosecutors on Tuesday unsealed terrorism charges against three men accused of supporting Iran’s Revolutionary Guards and Kata’ib Hizballah. The Justice Department alleges involvement in nearly 20 attacks and attempted attacks in Europe and the United States.

The defendants are Mohammad Baqer Saad Dawood al-Saadi, Mohsen Mohajeri and Mohammad Ali al-Hassani. The indictment includes a September plot at RAF Fairford in England, which housed US forces at the time. Al-Saadi was already jailed in New York.

Prosecutors allege that vans disguised as commercial vehicles approached RAF Fairford and at least two were stopped at a roadblock. They say al-Saadi passed coded messages from jail through his family, including his mother and sister. According to the indictment, Mohajeri and al-Hassani were in Iran during the alleged plot.

Why it matters. Prosecutors link attacks on Jewish sites in London to an alleged plot against a base used by US bombers. Attorney General Todd Blanche said anyone plotting against bases housing American troops will be prosecuted. The charges are allegations, and all defendants are presumed innocent unless proven guilty.

03 / Deep dive

One story, explained.

Why Rubio Says Economic Pressure, Not Strikes, Is the Plan for Iran

Part 1

Secretary of State Marco Rubio told G7 allies in Paris on Tuesday that the United States does not need major combat operations against Iran. Reuters, via Al Monitor, reports that he said sanctions and the naval blockade are working so well. He rejected the idea that this amounts to a stalemate.

Part 2

Rubio said Iran faces an unprecedented inability to sell energy on global markets. The Korea Times reports that he warned the US military stands ready if Iran crosses red lines. He also said Iran’s conditions for a deal were absurd and not serious.

Part 3

European allies pushed for calm. French Foreign Minister Jean-Noel Barrot said the risk of a new escalation has not been completely ruled out. Britain’s foreign minister, Ed Miliband, urged a full reopening of the Strait of Hormuz. Inside the administration, views differ. A person familiar with the thinking said Vice President JD Vance prefers a deal, while Trump for now sees no workable deal. Trump has acknowledged the war was one reason for Republican midterm losses.

Part 4

Why it matters. Economic pressure has not resolved the standoff. Iran’s security chief Mohsen Rezaei denied that negotiations with Washington were taking place and said Hormuz would not reopen until Tehran’s conditions were met. Separately, Iran’s army said a tanker hit a mine and caught fire. UKMTO reported a tanker incident but did not confirm the mine claim or whether it involved the same vessel.
