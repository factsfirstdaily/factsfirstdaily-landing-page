Kilmar Abrego Garcia Leaves the United States for Spain

Kilmar Abrego Garcia left the United States for Spain on Tuesday. His lawyers and Trump administration officials confirmed the departure, NPR reports. His case became a flashpoint over Trump's immigration agenda, and he has since arrived in Spain, the BBC reports.

He flew to Spain on a commercial flight Tuesday night, according to U.S. officials cited by CBS News. The exact conditions of his departure were not immediately clear. A judge's order issued Friday remains sealed.

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said Abrego Garcia had self-deported, ABC News reports. DHS repeated allegations against him, including gang membership, which he denies. His attorney said he "left the United States a free man."

Why it matters. He was deported to a Salvadoran prison in March 2025 despite a 2019 order barring his removal there. A top administration official later called that removal an error. DHS said he departed under a removal order and will never be allowed back, CBS reports.

U.S. Jury Convicts Huawei of Fraud and Trade Secret Theft

A federal jury in Brooklyn convicted Huawei on Tuesday of multiple criminal charges. The charges included bank fraud, sanctions violations and conspiracy to steal trade secrets, CNBC reports. Three subsidiaries were also found guilty.

Jurors returned guilty verdicts on 10 of 12 counts but acquitted Huawei of racketeering conspiracy, the BBC reports. That charge required finding the firm acted like a criminal enterprise.

The Justice Department said Huawei misled banks about its activities in Iran, CNBC reports. Prosecutors cited alleged theft involving Cisco and T-Mobile. Huawei said the remaining charges were without legal or factual merit.

Why it matters. The charges were first disclosed in 2019. The Chinese government criticized what it called U.S. suppression of Chinese companies, the BBC reports. Sentencing is scheduled for Sept. 14, 2027, and Huawei faces possible fines and forfeiture.

China Opens a Probe Into Shanghai Party Chief Chen Jining

China's anti-corruption watchdog said on Tuesday it is investigating Shanghai party secretary Chen Jining. The watchdog cited suspected violations of party discipline and gave no further details, NPR reports, citing the AP. Chen sits on the Politburo.

Al Jazeera reports the probe concerns alleged serious violations of discipline and law, usually code for corruption. Shanghai's party leaders said they firmly supported the decision.

A Eurasia Group analyst said Chen is the fourth Politburo member to fall in the current cycle. Earlier purged members include Gen. Zhang Youxia and He Weidong. Chen, 62, once served as environment minister, Al Jazeera reports.

Why it matters. The party will hold its next congress in the second half of 2027, Al Jazeera reports. President Xi Jinping is expected to secure a fourth term there. Experts say his purges are on a scale not seen since Mao Zedong.

Colombian Forces Free Hostages After a 17-Hour Supermarket Siege

Colombian security forces stormed a supermarket in Cartagena early Wednesday and freed the hostages inside. President Abelardo de la Espriella said all hostages were rescued and one captor was killed, CBS News reports.

Gunmen entered the store on Tuesday morning after what local media called a failed armored car robbery, the BBC reports. Around 20 people were still being held when forces moved in.

Ten of roughly 30 people first held were released during the standoff, CBS reports. Police said officers used stun grenades to overpower the hostage takers, the BBC reports. Local media said the gunmen demanded nearly $2 million and armored vehicles.

Why it matters. Cartagena is one of Colombia's main tourist destinations. The president said the gunmen had no political affiliations, the BBC reports. Cartagena's mayor said the hostages were safe and would get medical checks.

What Leaked Records Reveal About Russia's War Dead

What happened

At least 490,334 Russian soldiers have died since the 2022 invasion of Ukraine, according to government statistics analyzed by the BBC. The data came from leaked records of Rosstat, Russia's statistics agency. Russia does not publish its war losses. Two years ago Rosstat stopped publishing data on deaths from external causes.

Why it matters

BBC Russian accessed more than 11 million death records after a hack by Belarusian and Ukrainian groups. Rosstat admitted a cyberattack but denied protected information leaked. The BBC and Mediazona matched 90% of their own confirmed records. Experts estimate the true toll could range from 527,295 to 644,471.

What each side says

The records show 65% of Russians aged 18 to 24 who died in 2025 died in the war. Some 20,000 soldiers died fighting for 10 Ukrainian villages. Those villages had a combined pre-war population of 12,000. The average age of those killed rose from 34 in the first year to 40 by 2025.

What comes next

The figures land as both sides escalate. Ukraine said Putin could stop its refinery strikes by ending the war, CNBC reports. Putin said retaliation was inevitable. Kyiv's foreign minister claimed 432,000 Russian losses this year, which CNBC could not verify. Zelensky put Ukraine's own losses at about 50,000 in July 2026, the BBC reports.
