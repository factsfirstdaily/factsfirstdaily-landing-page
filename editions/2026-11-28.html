Breach Disrupts the Air Force's Flagship Cloud Platform

Hackers have breached Cloud One, the U.S. Air Force flagship cloud computing platform, three people familiar with the matter told Reuters, CNBC reports. The intrusion was discovered within the past month or so during upgrade work, two of the people said.

Reuters could not establish who the hackers were, the full scope of the intrusion or whether any data was stolen. One source said investigators have called in Cyber Command to assess the damage.

An Air Force spokesperson did not confirm or deny the breach, CBS News reports. The service said it does not comment on the security of specific networks. The Defense Department said its systems are safe and secure. Contractor Leidos deferred to the Air Force.

Why it matters. Sources told Reuters that application updates were blocked and some users lost administrator rights, CNBC reports. CBS News reports the alleged incident follows reported breaches and outages this month at agencies including the NSA, CIA, FBI and FAA. White House National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross was fired last week, CBS reports.

OpenAI Accuses Apple of Trapping Departing Workers

The ChatGPT maker answered Apple's lawsuit with counterclaims in a California federal court Friday, CNBC reports. OpenAI says Apple stole its trade secrets and steered hardware suppliers away from future OpenAI devices.

OpenAI alleges Apple sets up departing employees to be accused of stealing proprietary information, CBS News reports. The filing calls Apple's employment practices a trap that can be sprung to slow a potential competitor. OpenAI said 400 employees have left Apple for OpenAI so far, CNBC reports.

OpenAI also said Apple gained confidential information by rehiring a former employee who had worked at OpenAI for six months. Apple denied using the rehired employee to access that information. It also denied trying to suppress competition in AI devices, CNBC reports.

Why it matters. In a statement to CBS News, Apple dismissed the counterclaims as an attempt to distract from its own lawsuit. Its suit, filed earlier this year, targets OpenAI and two former employees over alleged trade secret theft. Analysts expect OpenAI to move into hardware, putting it in more direct competition with Apple.

Afghan Officials Link Deadly School Blast to Mining Explosives

At least 10 students died Saturday when an explosion struck near a school in eastern Afghanistan, an interior ministry spokesperson told the BBC. So far, 110 others have been reported injured. The blast hit the Anderseir area of Asadabad, the capital of eastern Kunar province.

Interior ministry spokesperson Abdul Mateen Qani said initial investigations pointed to negligence at an explosives depot belonging to nearby mining companies. He said an investigation was ongoing. The provincial hospital chief earlier gave Al Jazeera a toll of six dead and 97 injured.

A local information official told Al Jazeera the explosion occurred in a house beside a middle school at about 10 a.m.. He said the house belonged to people involved in mining who had stored dynamite there. Investigative teams were at the scene.

Why it matters. Mining is a key industry in Afghanistan, but safety standards at mining companies are often poor, Al Jazeera reports. Gemstone mining is on the rise in Kunar, which borders Pakistan. The hospital chief said casualty figures were initial and could change.

Two MSF Watchmen Killed in Israeli Strike on Gaza Clinic

Two workers for Medecins Sans Frontieres were killed in an Israeli strike in Gaza, the charity said, according to the BBC. MSF said the strike was a direct hit on one of its clinics in Gaza City.

MSF said the two staff killed were watchmen, Al Jazeera reports. The charity said its clinics carried MSF logos and that the Israeli military had been told where they were. MSF has now lost 17 staff members in Gaza, the group said.

The Israel Defense Forces described the two men as Hamas operatives working as guards at the clinic, the BBC reports. The military said its strike did not target a hospital. Al Jazeera reports Israel provided no evidence for its claim.

Why it matters. World Health Organization head Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus said attacks on Gaza's health workers and facilities were outrageous and unacceptable, the BBC reports. At least 1,705 people have been killed in Gaza over the past 13 months, the Hamas-run health ministry says. Violence has continued despite a U.S.-brokered ceasefire.

Spain's Sanchez Faces Voters as Polls Favor the Right

What happened

Spain holds a general election on Sunday, and party leaders held their final rallies on Friday, the BBC reports. Socialist Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez said he is confident of defying the polls. Almost 39 million people can vote. His conservative rival, Alberto Nunez Feijoo, promised a new era of truth, decency and good governance, the BBC reports.

Why it matters

Polls suggest the conservative People's Party will win most seats but need the far-right Vox party for a majority, Al Jazeera reports. A majority takes 176 seats in the 350-seat Congress of Deputies. Spanish electoral law has barred publishing new polls since Monday, the BBC reports.

What each side says

Sanchez called the snap election after lawmakers rejected two government decrees to broaden housing protections, CNBC reports. The campaign has centered on housing costs and everyday prices. Home purchase prices nearly doubled in the decade to the second quarter of 2026, according to Eurostat. CNBC reports Spanish inflation cooled to 4.1% in November, according to a flash estimate published Friday.

What comes next

CNBC reports a People's Party government could shift Spain's approach to Washington, Beijing and NATO. Sanchez and President Trump have sparred over Spanish bases, Gaza and NATO spending. If Vox joins a government, it would be the first far-right party to do so since dictator Francisco Franco died in 1975, CNBC reports.
