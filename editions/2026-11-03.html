America Votes Today. The U.S. Just Bombed Iran. And a Disease We Thought Was Gone Killed a Child.

01 / The Headlines

The main stories.

Nobody Knows Who Controls Congress After Tonight

Voting wraps up today in midterm elections that will set the balance of power in Washington for the last two years of President Trump's term. It's the most expensive campaign in U.S. history, and a record number of lawmakers are stepping down. Races in states few expected to matter, like Arkansas, Kansas, Mississippi and Iowa, could help decide control of Congress. Some results may take days.

Source: NPR

The U.S. Just Bombed Iran’s Key Launch Island

The U.S. carried out what it called defensive strikes overnight on Iranian targets in the strait, including Qeshm Island, a key launch point for Iran's attacks on shipping, the Associated Press reports. Iran reported explosions on the island. Tehran says it prefers talks and has offered to reopen the strait faster if Washington returns to negotiations.

Source: AP via NPR

A Child Just Died of Measles in New York — the First Death in 30 Years

A child in Lewis County, in the state's rural north, has died after contracting measles. Governor Kathy Hochul declared a measles disaster emergency last month as cases spread through rural counties.

Source: CNN, NBC News

Marvell Just Made a $115 Billion Bet on the AI Chip Race

Marvell agreed to buy Lumentum, maker of optical parts that power AI data centers, in one of the biggest tech deals on record. The deal signals how far chipmakers will go to own the infrastructure behind the AI boom. Lumentum's parts are in nearly every major cloud provider's network.

Source: TechCrunch

Texas Wildfires Are Spreading Fast as an Arctic Front Dries Out the Panhandle

Wildfire risk is surging across the Texas Panhandle and Southeast Texas as an Arctic front drives drought conditions and high winds. The Texas A&M Forest Service is deploying additional crews. Farms, ranches and rural communities are on alert as dry conditions are expected to persist through the week.

Source: ABC13 Houston, RFD-TV

02 / The Context

The background.

E-Bike Crashes Are Sending More Kids to the ER Than Ever

What happened

A Stanford Medicine-led study of records from six U.S. children's hospitals found e-bike injuries among young riders grew about 33% every quarter. E-scooter injuries rose about 15% a quarter.

Why it matters

E-bikes are heavier and faster than regular bikes, and doctors say the crashes tend to cause more severe injuries than scooter crashes.

What to watch

Researchers are pushing for clearer rules on rider age and speed. For families: a helmet every ride, practice on a regular bike first, and no phones or earbuds while riding.

Source: Stanford Medicine
