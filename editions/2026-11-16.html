Anthropic's IPO Week Becomes a Test for the Whole AI Industry

Anthropic, the maker of the Claude chatbot, is expected to debut on the Nasdaq on Thursday. CNBC reports the offering matters to almost every major tech company. Shares are expected to price at $70 to $78.

The offering could raise over $90 billion. CNBC reports the company is eyeing a valuation of roughly $1.4 trillion. It already holds $132 billion in cash, including short-term investments.

Seven chip and cloud giants, including Nvidia, Google and Microsoft, are buying $26 billion of stock at the IPO price. Anthropic lists over $900 billion in spending commitments in its prospectus. It posted a net loss of $104 billion through nine months.

Why it matters. The sale could show whether investors will keep funding the costly AI buildout. CNBC reports traders are also awaiting Nvidia's earnings after the bell on Tuesday. Nasdaq-100 futures were down 0.79% on CNBC's live page at about 6:07 a.m. ET Monday.

Senator Cantwell Presses Lutnick to Stop AI Chip Sales to China

Sen. Maria Cantwell sent Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick a letter on Sunday about AI chip exports. CNBC reports she alleged his department failed to stop China from getting the most advanced U.S. chips.

Cantwell is the top Democrat on the Senate commerce committee. She urged Lutnick to enforce a 2018 export control law. She also cited a 25% fee on certain Nvidia chips sold to China. She said it could create a perverse incentive to approve exports.

The pressure follows Jensen Huang's resignation as Nvidia chief. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent called for a dramatic shakeup at Nvidia on Sunday. He questioned whether the company pursued potentially illicit chip sales to lift earnings.

Why it matters. Export controls are meant to protect America's lead in artificial intelligence. Lutnick has agreed to testify before the Senate banking committee in December. The Commerce Department did not immediately respond to CNBC on Monday.

Four Allies Urge Israel to Let 150,000 Tents Into Gaza Before Winter

The foreign ministers of Australia, Canada, Italy and the United Kingdom issued a joint appeal on Sunday. Al Jazeera reports they said 150,000 tents and other vital supplies are being held at Gaza's border.

The ministers urged Israel to stop the delays and allow rapid, unimpeded aid access. BBC News reports the statement says the crossings must be opened and the tents allowed in. Israel's military authority for the crossings says 93,000 tents have already entered Gaza.

Heavy rain this month swept away tens of thousands of tents in Gaza. Al Jazeera reports more than 1.9 million people are displaced, according to the United Nations. A shelter group estimated residents need at least 121,000 tents.

Why it matters. Winter is setting in for families with little protection from cold and rain. The European Union and a UN team issued similar warnings in recent days. The ministers said there is still time to act.

Voyager 1 Is About to Become the First Craft One Light-Day From Earth

NASA's Voyager 1 is set to reach a new milestone on Wednesday. ABC News reports it will be one light-day from Earth at 5:16 a.m. ET. It will be the first human-made object that far away.

A light-day is the distance light travels in 24 hours, more than 16 billion miles. The probe is traveling at about 38,000 mph. It launched in 1977 on what was meant to be a five-year mission.

Voyager 1 flew past Jupiter and Saturn and found a faint ring system around Jupiter. NPR reports NASA is asking the public to flash the probe's call sign, VGR, in Morse code on Tuesday night.

Why it matters. The aging probe still sends data from interstellar space nearly 50 years after launch. Voyager 1 and its twin are the only spacecraft to have operated outside the heliosphere. That bubble shields the solar system.

What Yemen's Retreat From the Bab Al-Mandab Means for Shipping

What happened

Saudi-backed Yemeni forces said Sunday they were withdrawing from an area overlooking the Bab al-Mandab Strait, a key maritime choke point. BBC News reports the redeployment would reinforce Aden. Houthi-run media say the group has taken control of the evacuated territory. The Houthis seized ground near the strait in a surprise offensive in September. Government forces launched a campaign in October to take it back. The withdrawal ends that weeks-long offensive.

Why it matters

Reuters says the retreat gives the Houthis greater leverage over a key maritime choke point. Saudi Arabia has relied on Red Sea routes for oil exports since the Strait of Hormuz effectively closed. Iran is still disrupting traffic through Hormuz on the other side of the Arabian Peninsula.

What each side says

The Southern Armed Forces said on Sunday that troops were leaving Dhubab Airport and the strait area. NBC News, carrying a Reuters report, says they are redeploying toward Aden, where the government is based. The Houthis say they pose no threat to international shipping.

What comes next

The Southern Armed Forces say securing Aden is now their priority. The Houthis say they will keep targeting Saudi vessels. They have repeatedly attacked Aden in recent weeks. The UN estimates nearly 330,000 people have been displaced as fighting escalated.
